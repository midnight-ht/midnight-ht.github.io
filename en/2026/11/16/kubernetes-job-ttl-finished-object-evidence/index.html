When does Kubernetes start the TTL for a finished Job?

Calculate the eligibility time from two terminal Job states and preserve evidence before Kubernetes cleanup.

M Midnight Engineering notes on development, quality, security, delivery, and operations. View Author Profile
Updated 2026-11-16 · Estimated read 9 min
Translations
zh-CN en

Article

Key takeaways

  • The TTL starts at a terminal state.
  • Expiry makes cleanup eligible rather than immediate.
  • Retain terminal status and application receipts separately.

The TTL starts from a Complete or Failed condition. Expiry makes the Kubernetes Job eligible for cleanup. Export the evidence you need while you can still retrieve it, and calculate the wait from the terminal state.

An operator sees a batch task finish and plans to inspect its logs later. The Job and its Pod are gone when they return. Creation time and the TTL field do not give the cleanup boundary for that run. Find the terminal condition and keep cleanup, object disappearance, and the application result separate.

Calculate eligibility from the terminal condition

The Kubernetes cleanup documentation says the controller starts counting when the Job shows Complete or Failed. After the configured time, it can delete the Job and its dependents together. Kubernetes still honors finalizers. Record the terminal timestamp and the eventual disappearance separately.

Suppose a Job is created at second zero, completes at twelve, and has a thirty-second TTL. It becomes eligible at forty-two. A separate Job created at ten and failed at seventeen becomes eligible at forty-seven. A task can still be running thirty seconds after creation. That time alone does not trigger this cleanup. These times are illustrative, without promising deletion at a precise second.

Observe a success and a deliberate failure

This complete manifest was not run. Use an isolated test namespace. One process prints a success marker. The other prints a distinct marker and fails intentionally. Both sleep for forty-five seconds, so their run duration can exceed the TTL without any terminal state. The failed Job has no extra retry budget.

apiVersion: batch/v1
kind: Job
metadata:
  name: ttl-lab-ok
spec:
  ttlSecondsAfterFinished: 30
  backoffLimit: 0
  template:
    spec:
      restartPolicy: Never
      containers:
        - name: worker
          image: busybox:1.36.1
          command: ["sh", "-c", "sleep 45; echo receipt-ok"]
---
apiVersion: batch/v1
kind: Job
metadata:
  name: ttl-lab-fail
spec:
  ttlSecondsAfterFinished: 30
  backoffLimit: 0
  template:
    spec:
      restartPolicy: Never
      containers:
        - name: worker
          image: busybox:1.36.1
          command: ["sh", "-c", "sleep 45; echo receipt-fail; exit 7"]

Set up observation before applying the manifest. Record each Pod’s UID, start time and output. Capture both terminal conditions and their timestamps, then add the configured TTL to each. Observe eventual cleanup without treating a brief delay as a contradictory result. Check ownership when looking for the dependent Pod. A later Job may reuse the name. If the terminal evidence is missing, record that gap instead of inferring success from the absent object.

An otherwise equivalent Job without a TTL can act as a control if no other cleanup policy interferes. A running Job that has exceeded the TTL duration since creation is another control. See the Job active deadline for a bound on an active task rather than on a finished object.

Give evidence its own lifetime

The terminal condition explains the cleanup decision. A durable application receipt answers whether a report or other deliverable was written. Where operators need to investigate later, keep the relevant output and identifiers away from the lifecycle of the Job object. These teaching markers contain no externally persisted result.

Verify the observation path before attributing missing logs to cleanup. A delayed collector can produce an empty archive while the Pod still exists. Capture the source observation and the archival outcome separately. Then decide whether the retention setting or the collection path needs to change.

Record both Job and Pod UIDs before comparing times. The container can exit before the manager records the terminal Job condition. A later run can also reuse the printed name. Match the durable receipt to this specific run, then check retrieval after cleanup if that is a release requirement. These are proposed checks.

Does extending the TTL after expiry guarantee that the Job stays?

The manual explicitly says no. A successful update can arrive after the previous TTL has already expired and does not guarantee retention. That race is separate from the main fixture, which does not change the TTL after completion. Use an independent store for any evidence that must remain available.
