Sprint Review does not replace Done or act as a release gate

Separate Done, Review and delivery with three report states, including the rule for unfinished work and shared organizational standards.

M Midnight Engineering notes on development, quality, security, delivery, and operations. View Author Profile
Updated 2026-11-10 · Estimated read 13 min
Translations
zh-CN en

Article

Key takeaways

  • Review does not grant Increment status to unfinished work.
  • A usable Increment may be delivered before the Sprint ends.
  • Organizational minimums and a shared product DoD remain explicit commitments.

An Increment meeting the Definition of Done may be delivered before the Sprint ends. Work that does not meet it cannot be released or presented at Sprint Review. The meeting does not grant Increment status. A single “completed” label can obscure three separate facts: a demonstration occurred, quality measures were met, and delivery happened.

Consider a hypothetical department report. Its screen opens, the prepared demonstration data looks convincing, and stakeholders like the direction. Authorization is not connected yet, however, and integration with real data has not been verified. The demonstration says something about the proposed interface. It does not establish that the product’s quality commitment has been met. This is a teaching scenario, not an account of an actual team or Sprint acceptance.

Two mistakes point in opposite directions

The Increment section of the November 2020 Scrum Guide requires an Increment to be usable, additive to previous Increments, and thoroughly verified so that they work together. A new screen working on its own is a smaller claim. In the report example, opening the page after login and limiting its data to the authorized department are separate requirements.

The opposite mistake is to hold usable, Done work until the Review because the meeting supposedly grants permission to deliver. The Guide allows multiple Increments within a Sprint and delivery before its end. Sprint Review must not become a gate to releasing value. That statement removes a supposed framework restriction; it does not require every product to ignore its own release arrangements.

Nor does “Review is not a gate” authorize releasing unfinished work. Quality measures and delivery timing need separate answers. An organization’s applicable release controls, safety checks, and evidence of user access remain distinct. This article does not approve any particular release.

Classify the same result without hiding its state

For this example, suppose the agreed quality measures require authorized real data, correct department access, and verified integration. These are proposed measures for the imaginary report, not fields the Guide mandates for every product.

Item Quality state Delivery fact Independent classification
A Screen opens; authorization and integration remain unfinished Not delivered Not Done; not an Increment
B All agreed measures met Not yet delivered A usable Increment; delivery is separate
C All agreed measures met Delivered before Review A usable Increment already delivered

Derive the classifications from the commitment and the Guide, rather than from the board color. A cannot be released or presented at Sprint Review; it returns to the Product Backlog for future consideration. B does not become unfinished merely because public delivery is still pending. C need not wait for Review to acquire permission to deliver. Review still inspects results and informs adaptation. These are normative expectations, not observed execution results.

A useful negative case is leaving A marked Done while adding “authorization to follow after Review.” The missing work still exists; only the label has become ambiguous. If the team discovers that an important quality measure was absent from its original definition, it should make that discovery visible, decide the work required, and assess affected results. A meeting cannot retroactively supply missing evidence.

What Review inspects and what it cannot grant

The Sprint Review section places the purpose in inspecting the Sprint’s outcome and determining future adaptations. Participants discuss what was accomplished, what changed in their environment, and what to do next. The Product Backlog may change. This is a working session, rather than merely a scripted presentation.

Quality problems can certainly be inspected and discussed. Saying that Review does not replace the Definition of Done does not prohibit reporting defects or require everyone to deliver good news. The substitution to avoid is treating stakeholder agreement as proof that an unmet quality measure has become satisfied. Discovering a problem, scheduling its correction, and completing the correction are different facts.

Stakeholders may also ask report B to use a newly requested reporting period. The team should distinguish an existing commitment that was missed from a new adaptation learned through discussion. The former can affect the assessment of current quality; the latter can become new backlog work. Calling both “later improvements” simply to preserve a completion percentage hides the decision. Turning an export requirement into observable outcomes develops the business-rule discussion; it does not replace this framework distinction.

Delivery still deserves its own evidence

Record who received which result and whether it was usable in that context. Meeting the Definition of Done does not generate a deployment receipt or a user-access record. If an applicable release window remains, “usable Increment, delivery awaiting the window” communicates more than quietly returning the item to a vague development state.

This need not become an elaborate state machine. The record should answer which measures were met and what was delivered to whom. For software, artifact, deployment and rollback verification adds delivery evidence. It does not assign Sprint Review a universal deployment-approval role.

Can a team lower an organizational DoD to meet a date?

The Definition of Done commitment makes organizational standards a minimum for all Scrum Teams. A team may add appropriate product measures. It cannot silently use a lower standard for this Sprint while continuing to claim the same Done state.

Standards can be reconsidered, but changing a standard and concealing an exception for one item are different decisions. A deadline may lead to scope negotiation; it should not be satisfied by making the quality label less informative.

Can teams on one product use separate Done standards?

The Guide requires them to mutually define and comply with the same Definition of Done. Otherwise, one team’s completed contribution may still need another team to finish its integration, undermining a shared assessment of the product. Classify A, B and C against the same measures before discussing cadence. If teams disagree on those classifications, settle the standard before comparing their completion percentages.
