Product validation 2026-11-08

Was the new design faster, or had users just learned the task?

Faster second attempts may reflect learning rather than a better interface. A four-sequence plan for two designs and two materials makes order checkable and identifies when separate participants are preferable.

M Midnight Engineering notes on development, quality, security, delivery, and operations. View Author Profile
Updated 2026-11-08 · Estimated read 13 min
Translations
zh-CN en

Article

Key takeaways

  • Track interface, material and presentation position separately.
  • Counterbalancing does not erase arbitrary irreversible learning.
  • Retain failures and assistance rather than comparing successful times alone.

When everyone uses the old version first, a faster second attempt cannot be attributed to the interface alone. Treat order as an input to the study. Reusing participants reduces some person-level variation while carrying knowledge from the first condition into the second; the same participant does not automatically make a fair comparison.

NN/g’s original explanation of within- and between-subject designs, published on July 10, 2023, distinguishes exposing one person to all conditions from assigning different people to different conditions. It discusses learning, session length and assignment. These distinctions do not establish a universal participant count or a power guarantee.

The first attempt can teach the business task

Suppose two prototypes support appointment booking. On the first attempt, participants learn that they must choose a service, prepare a document and then select a time. They bring that knowledge to the second interface. Less searching may reflect task learning rather than better navigation.

Transfer includes terminology, material reading and understanding the endpoint, not just button locations. Fatigue may instead make the second attempt slower. If everyone sees the same version order, interface and position are confounded.

The existing neutral-task article warns against giving away routes and mistaking rehearsal for usability. This question goes further within one comparative study: the first interface can teach participants even when the moderator supplies no hints. Auditing the discussion guide alone cannot control that source.

Separate interface, material and position

The following is an unexecuted teaching plan, with no recruited participants. Interfaces A and B address the same booking goal. Materials X and Y should have comparable information load, business complexity and correct endpoints, but different teaching names, dates and document numbers. Equivalence requires a pilot; equal page counts do not establish it.

Planned sequence First attempt Second attempt
1 A with X B with Y
2 B with Y A with X
3 A with Y B with X
4 B with X A with Y

A and B each appear twice in each position. Each interface-material combination appears twice. These are independently checkable properties of the matrix, not evidence that participant assignment occurred. Before actual sessions, define the assignment procedure and randomly allocate people to the planned sequences. Record absences and rescheduling.

Putting all weekend sessions into AB and weekdays into BA is not random assignment. Availability can identify different populations. The study-design source explicitly discusses this calendar confound. Equal sequence counts do not remove systematic links with date, moderator or user type.

A counterexample with an independent expected result

Assume both interfaces have an identical 100-second teaching baseline, and completing any task second creates a 20-second learning reduction. There is no interface effect. An all-A-then-B schedule would show A at 100 seconds and B at 80, inventing a 20-second advantage.

Under the symmetric additive model, the four-sequence plan puts each interface first half the time and second half the time. Both average 90 seconds. This is arithmetic over hypothetical inputs, not participant data or an observed improvement. It demonstrates balance for that particular position effect, not removal of arbitrary carryover.

A might reveal the answer in a way that helps only a subsequent B attempt. Such asymmetric transfer need not cancel when order is reversed. Materials may also interact with an interface: X might contain a harder-to-read document than Y. Revisit the goal, adjust materials or use separate participants rather than treating the matrix as a validity certificate.

Record enough to interpret the comparison

Use the GOV.UK moderated-testing guidance to prepare credible goals without revealing interface routes. Define the correct business endpoint and pilot the materials. Record participant, interface, material, position, assistance and outcome, with consistent timing boundaries.

A prompted second attempt is not directly comparable with an independent first attempt. Excluding failures and comparing only successful times can also change the population. Retain failures, withdrawals and assistance; a faster incorrect booking is not success.

Inspect each interface by first and second position before interpreting an overall difference. Check whether the apparent advantage reverses with order, then inspect material and moderator conditions. Small studies may expose design problems without supporting a population speed claim. Splitting by order is not permission for unlimited significance testing.

Can reversing order restore a novice comparison after the first design teaches the answer?

Not reliably. If the target is first use and exposure irreversibly reveals the rule, consider a between-subject design: randomly assign different participants to one interface each. It introduces person-level variation and potentially more recruitment, but does not require people to unlearn the task.

NN/g’s validity discussion recommends counterbalancing or randomizing condition order in within-subject comparisons while keeping the procedure sensible. Mechanically switching sites after every action may distort the task. Grouping tasks by site and balancing site order can be more appropriate.

Choose the design from transfer, repeatability, session burden and the intended estimand. Qualitative barrier discovery need not become a speed contest; reporting the step requiring assistance can be more defensible than declaring a winner.

Proposed validation first checks matrix counts, then the 100-second baseline and 20-second learning counterexample. Actual research needs assignment, pilot, assistance and failure records. Participants’ own devices and environments remain a separate boundary: order control does not make laboratory findings cover everyday work. With no sessions executed, the deliverable is the design and its pending checks.
