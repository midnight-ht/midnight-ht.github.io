Conversion rose, but why did a 50/50 test become 60/40?

When analyzed group counts disagree with planned allocation, reconcile assignment, exposure and inclusion before interpreting lift. A hypothetical ledger separates SRM detection, diagnosis and the benefit decision.

M Midnight Engineering notes on development, quality, security, delivery, and operations. View Author Profile
Updated 2026-11-08 · Estimated read 13 min
Translations
zh-CN en

Article

Key takeaways

  • Compute expected counts from the actual allocation and experimental unit.
  • SRM is a diagnostic signal rather than proof of a particular cause.
  • Repair inclusion before deciding whether to recompute or rerun.

The plan assigns half the users to each variant, while the report retains 600 and 400. Find where users disappeared before interpreting the outcome difference. Sample ratio mismatch, or SRM, is a signal to investigate the data; it does not identify a faulty logger, prove broken randomization or establish that a variant is harmful.

Microsoft Research’s original SRM diagnostic article places the check before effect interpretation. Observed inclusion counts are compared with configured allocation, with sample size taken into account. Published on September 14, 2020, the article describes Microsoft’s practice. Its organizational threshold is not a universal product requirement.

Correct percentages can still describe incomparable groups

Suppose each variant records 120 completed tasks. Against 600 analyzed users, A converts at 20%; against 400, B converts at 30%. Both calculations are correct. If B disproportionately loses users who did not complete, however, its larger percentage may reflect missing denominators rather than easier task completion.

This adds a separate question to defining a product metric’s denominator. A team can already agree on unique users, a completion condition and a common window, yet retain users asymmetrically. Random assignment at the beginning does not guarantee comparability after treatment-dependent filtering.

Unequal counts alone do not establish an incident. A 50/50 allocation permits chance variation; a planned 60/40 allocation does not expect equality. A usable check requires configured shares, observed counts, the randomization unit and a defined window. A screenshot showing 60/40 lacks enough information either to approve the analysis or to condemn the assignment algorithm.

Reconcile four stages before naming a cause

The following is a complete hypothetical teaching ledger, not a platform query or a real experiment. Each stage is a subset of the preceding population within the same window.

Stage Control A Treatment B
Unique users assigned 1000 1000
Reached the common task entry 800 800
Retained for analysis 600 400
Recorded completion 120 120

The table directs attention between common entry and analysis, but it does not identify which operation caused the asymmetry. Several causes could coexist. Preserve exclusion reasons, identity and deduplication rules, and allocation versions before attributing the discrepancy to a particular processing step.

The original article distinguishes assignment, execution, log processing and analysis as potential sources. Triggering conditions deserve particular care. Filtering for people who used a newly introduced coupon field may provide no equivalent event in the control. Giving two different behaviors the same event name does not create a valid comparison. Define a common inclusion condition or appropriate counterfactual logging, then verify who could have encountered the change.

Recompute the check for 1,000 analyzed users

Under a fixed 50/50 plan, the expected analysis counts are 500 and 500. Each observed deviation is 100, so each chi-square contribution is 100 squared divided by 500, or 20. The total is 40.

This Python script remains unexecuted. It calculates only the allocation check for the two-category fixture; it does not connect to an experiment platform or test conversion lift.

import math

observed = [600, 400]
shares = [0.5, 0.5]
expected = [sum(observed) * share for share in shares]
chi2 = sum((actual - mean) ** 2 / mean
           for actual, mean in zip(observed, expected))
p = math.erfc(math.sqrt(chi2 / 2))  # chi-square survival for df=1
assert expected == [500.0, 500.0]
assert chi2 == 40.0
assert 2.5e-10 < p < 2.6e-10
print({'expected': expected, 'chi2': chi2, 'p': p})

The expected statistic comes from the independent arithmetic above. For one degree of freedom, the written complementary-error-function identity gives a tail probability of approximately 2.54 times ten to the minus ten. The approximation has a clear teaching scope: two fixed allocation categories with adequate expected counts. Small counts, cluster assignment or changing allocation probabilities need an appropriate design instead.

A check that finds no mismatch establishes much less than complete validity. Equal-sized groups could record different completion definitions or lose the same kind of user. SRM does not replace outcome, harm or randomization-unit checks.

Repair inclusion rather than the appearance of balance

Suspend the benefit conclusion, retain original counts and configuration, and investigate justified segments such as entry route, device or time period. If assignment and common exposure remain balanced but filtering introduces a mismatch, inspect that condition and logging symmetry. A segment can help diagnosis; selecting a favorable segment afterward does not restore the planned population claim.

Deleting surplus control observations can manufacture equal counts without recovering missing treatment users. Weighting also requires defensible knowledge of selection and missingness. Decide whether trustworthy raw events support recomputation or whether the experiment must be rerun, considering recoverability, configuration changes and the original analysis plan.

The related canary comparison article addresses shared harm that disappears in a relative difference. That user-impact boundary is separate from this population-generation check. Neither can be omitted because the other appears normal.

Should a planned 60/40 experiment be forced to report 50/50?

No. Expected counts must follow the allocation actually active in the window. With 1,000 analyzed users under a 60/40 contract, the expectation is 600 and 400. Testing against equality would test the wrong hypothesis. During ramps, retain configuration-specific expectations rather than applying the final allocation to all historical enrollment.

Proposed validation should include a balanced fixture, the mismatched ledger and a deliberately planned 60/40 fixture. Check the configuration read by the diagnostic before checking its arithmetic and resulting decision. A real product also needs reconciled identities and windows across assignment, exposure, inclusion and completion. Until exclusion and recovery are understood, the benefit conclusion remains unknown.
