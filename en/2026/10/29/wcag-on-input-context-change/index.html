WCAG On Input: when must a navigation warning appear?

Distinguish focus, ordinary activation, and setting changes in a housing form, including state-changing buttons, advance advice, local updates, and recovery.

M Midnight Engineering notes on development, quality, security, delivery, and operations. View Author Profile
Updated 2026-10-29 · Estimated read 12 min
Translations
zh-CN en

Article

Key takeaways

  • Advice must precede use when that condition is relied upon.
  • A local update is not automatically a context change.
  • Distinguish ordinary activation from a button’s setting change.

An applicant selects housing assistance and is immediately sent to another form. Only the destination explains that this choice switches application processes. The explanation arrives after the action. WCAG’s On Input criterion asks whether users could anticipate the change before operating the control; a destination-page notice cannot retroactively become prior advice.

A setting change is different from receiving focus

The WCAG 2.2 explanation of On Input describes SC 3.2.2, a Level A criterion. Changing a component’s setting does not automatically cause a change of context unless users were advised of that behavior before using it. Checking a box, entering text, and changing a selected option change settings. Activating an ordinary link, submitting a form with navigation, or using a button to move focus is activation. Buttons are not universally excluded: the explanation specifically treats toggling aria-pressed and becoming disabled on activation as setting changes. Classify the behavior rather than the element name.

Do not transfer this condition to the neighboring On Focus criterion. Reaching the housing option can simply mean reading it; changing its value introduces the trigger discussed here. The earlier On Focus article already addressed why a warning does not create an exception for focus-triggered navigation. The additional questions here concern prior advice for an actual setting change, dependent fields, and the choice of a separate action.

Not every content update is a context change. Revealing related fields in place differs from opening a window, navigating to another application, or moving focus elsewhere. A network request alone establishes neither result. Nor does an unchanged URL prove users remain at their previous operating position. Inspect the actual viewport, focus, and substantial changes in content meaning.

Three paths for the same housing option

Consider an unimplemented, untested assistance application. Users have entered a phone number and email address before selecting housing assistance.

The first design reveals room type and housing dates in place. Focus remains on the original option and contact details remain available. Assess whether this is an understandable local update before requiring an additional confirmation button.

The second design immediately navigates to another application. If this path is retained, advise users before they operate the option. Wording, placement, and association must give them an opportunity to encounter the consequence beforehand. An explanation beneath the destination heading arrives too late. I prefer an explicit action for consequential switches, but that recommendation does not remove the criterion’s prior-advice condition.

The third design lets the selection prepare a choice and uses “Continue to housing application” to initiate navigation. It adds an action but allows inspection, revision, and exit. Check the original change handler: a new button is ineffective if selecting the option still triggers the old navigation first.

Decide separately whether existing contact details transfer. This criterion does not guarantee saving, authorized reuse, or cross-system transmission. If the destination is unavailable, a service might preserve the current application for later or offer a clear return route. It must not claim information was saved without evidence of persistence.

Observe three triggers independently

Proposed verification begins with completed contact fields. Reach the housing option with Tab without changing it; observe navigation and focus. Change its value and record the result. Finally activate the explicit Continue action and inspect the destination. Separating these steps identifies whether focus, a setting change, or activation caused the transition.

When relying on prior advice, record the notice, its presentation time, and whether the target assistive technology exposes it before operation. No browser or screen-reader testing was conducted here. A sentence on a design file does not establish that users encountered or understood it. Research should also ask participants what they expect, rather than relying on reviewers who already know the process.

Inspect failures: option-loading errors, unexpected default selection, returning from the destination, and disagreement between the selected value and retained fields. These are proposed variants, not allegations about every component. Retain before-and-after viewport, focus, address, and field state. A destination screenshot cannot establish when advice was provided.

Also distinguish preview from business writes. If every selection already persists state, adding Continue does not undo earlier writes. The action’s promise, data persistence, and context changes need separate requirements.

Does live filtering always need a confirmation button?

No universal button rule follows merely from automatic updating. A product filter may update cards and result counts in place while preserving the user’s position. First establish whether context actually changes. Automatically opening the first product or moving focus to the top is a different outcome and needs a fresh assessment of consequences and prior advice.

My recommendation for in-place updates is to retain understandable loading, result, and empty states while users continue filtering. Announcing those states without taking focus is another accessibility question; satisfying On Input does not resolve it automatically.

For each option, record the triggering action, where the change occurs, where advance advice appears, and how failure can be recovered. That preserves useful dependent interfaces while exposing selections that quietly become commitments to another process.
