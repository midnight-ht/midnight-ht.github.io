Define incident severity from observable impact

Define incident impact, escalation and coordination without importing another team’s severity numbers.

M Midnight Engineering notes on development, quality, security, delivery, and operations. View Author Profile
Updated 2026-10-17 · Estimated read 6 min
Translations
zh-CN en

Article

Incident severity should connect observable user impact with response conditions. Alert volume or noise alone cannot determine it. Many alerts can describe one fault, while one quiet signal can indicate a failed critical journey. Classification exists to organize response rather than decorate a technical problem with a number.

Severity serves response

The PagerDuty incident-response handbook gives impact-based severity examples and recommends organization-specific, metric-driven definitions. Lower-severity issues can still need coordinated response. This is one organization's practice, not a universal industry standard; its numbers, actions and risk tolerance should not be copied automatically.

Imagine repeated alerts from a minor component while one signal indicates that many users cannot submit critical work. Sorting solely by alert count can misallocate responders. This is an unexecuted example, with no real incident, customer count or loss data.

Define impact, not only component names

Useful definitions address affected capabilities, user scope, duration, alternatives and uncertainty requiring escalation. Thresholds need actual service commitments and evidence; no universal percentage is proposed here. A modestly named internal component can sit on a critical path.

Define who can raise response intensity under uncertainty, record a provisional assessment and reassess it. Requiring complete proof before requesting help can delay action. Later evidence should not be represented as knowledge available earlier. Save timing and reasoning for review.

Connect levels to roles, coordination and update expectations. Tie response actions to your own roles, on-call arrangements and channels. The handbook's contact commands belong to its own workflow and are not transferable without the corresponding operational agreement.

Numbers should not block collaboration

An issue below the highest impact threshold may still require several teams. If coordination is available only at a particular number, responders can end up arguing classifications to obtain help. Give the responsible role room to coordinate from evidence and revise the assessment.

Recovery needs user-task, backlog and residual-risk checks rather than alert silence alone. Green metrics may indicate partial recovery while compensation remains. Persistent old alerts also need current interpretation rather than an assumption of unchanged impact.

Proposed tabletop cases cover critical-path failure, uncertain impact, lower-severity coordination and recovery with outstanding work. Record assessors, times and decisions to evaluate clarity. No exercise or organization's policy was validated here.

Incident command and handoff discusses coordination responsibilities. Tie classification to explainable impact and action while permitting timely help under uncertainty.
