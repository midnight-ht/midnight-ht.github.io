Explain the next state on a confirmation page

Explain confirmed submission, next steps and reliable revisit paths on receipt pages.

M Midnight Engineering notes on development, quality, security, delivery, and operations. View Author Profile
Updated 2026-10-17 · Estimated read 6 min
Translations
zh-CN en

Article

A confirmation page should identify the completed submission, explain what happens next and show how to track it or get help. A green panel alone does not explain whether an application was approved or whether evidence is still needed. Scope the success message to the operation actually confirmed.

Submission is not the entire task

The GOV.UK confirmation-page pattern recommends a reference where applicable, next steps and timing, contact details, relevant services and a way to save the transaction record. It also considers people bookmarking the page. This is design guidance, not evidence that adopting a component improves completion rates.

Imagine an application requiring later assessment. “Everything complete” might imply approval when the system has only received documents. This untested scenario calls for wording tied to confirmed facts: what was submitted and how a decision will be communicated. Any expected timeframe needs real operational support rather than a number invented to fill the page.

A reference should connect to a business record and a usable support lookup. Decide separately whether it can be public and how a requester is authenticated. Knowing a reference must not automatically expose personal information. Avoid unnecessary internal debugging identifiers.

Preserve meaning on a revisit

A bookmark may be the user’s future tracking entrance. An unexplained expired session or empty form can encourage another submission. Consider secure status lookup, a saved receipt or useful contact instructions. Data-access and retention requirements still apply; when the original page cannot be shown again, explain the available route.

Email can supplement confirmation, but do not claim delivery without corresponding evidence. Queued sending, service acceptance and receipt are different states. Resending needs limits, a verified destination and association with the current task rather than repeated attempts that hide a notification fault.

Give next-step information an owner

Timeframes, contacts and evidence requirements change with operations. Assign responsibility for updating them and deciding how changes affect earlier applicants. A template owner alone may deliver technically valid pages containing outdated advice. Include this information in change review and acceptance.

Proposed checks cover normal submission, revisits, unavailable notifications, reference lookup and requests for more information. Ask whether users can explain what is finished, what remains and whom to contact. No user research or notification tests were executed here.

Pre-submission review has a different responsibility: see editable check-answers pages. A useful confirmation lets people leave with a reliable record and find their next action when they return.
