The Backup Job Is Green: Why Does Recovery Still Fail?

Validate PostgreSQL 18 recovery through roles and restored business behavior instead of a successful backup-job status.

M Midnight Engineering notes on development, quality, security, delivery, and operations. View Author Profile
Updated 2026-10-09 · Estimated read 6 min
Translations
zh-CN en

Article

A successful backup job establishes a file or transfer step, not recoverable business operation. A PostgreSQL rehearsal must inspect coverage, roles, environmental dependencies, error handling, and restored outcomes. No backup was restored and no recovery duration was measured here.

A dump is not the whole environment

The PostgreSQL 18 SQL Dump documentation explains that pg_dump handles one database and omits cluster-wide roles and tablespaces. Plain SQL dumps are restored with psql; non-text dumps use pg_restore, and owners and granted roles must be available to reconstruct permissions properly.

A hypothetical team creates an empty database but omits roles needed by the dump. Normal file size and successful upload do not fix that. Inventory extensions, configuration, and external dependencies rather than calling one file a complete system backup.

Decide what happens after an error

For plain SQL restoration with psql, errors can be followed by continued execution by default. The documentation discusses stop-on-error and single-transaction options. Choose from scale and recovery policy, not the presence of a “finished” line.

Partial restoration may leave the application able to start while indexes, constraints, or permissions are missing. Validate critical objects and business queries alongside exit status and error categories. Protect sensitive logs and samples.

Confirm the application connects to the restored target with intended timezone, extensions, and settings. Record tool versions, backup time, environment, and differences so acceptance cannot accidentally validate another existing dataset.

End the clock at a business outcome

A candidate check retrieves a known completed order with its details and permissions, then exercises required writes and subsequent tasks. Use evidence-backed sample selection rather than one arbitrary existing row. Establish the acceptable recovery point with the business.

Recovery duration includes environment preparation, retrieval, dependency reconstruction, validation, and service cutover, not just import. Do not promise “minutes” without a rehearsal or generalize one measured run beyond its scale and conditions.

Plan for missing or damaged files, expired credentials, and unreachable storage. A digest helps detect transfer changes but does not prove logical completeness. None of these scenarios was executed here.

Restore a recent backup from scratch in an authorized controlled environment and record a business completion point. Keep restoration distinct from release rollback: restarting an old application does not prove it can read changed data.
