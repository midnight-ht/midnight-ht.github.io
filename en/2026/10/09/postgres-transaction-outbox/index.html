The Database Committed but the Notification Failed: Define the Handoff

Use a transactional outbox to make handoff recoverable while defining worker ownership and duplicate delivery handling.

M Midnight Engineering notes on development, quality, security, delivery, and operations. View Author Profile
Updated 2026-10-09 · Estimated read 6 min
Translations
zh-CN en

Article

Do not rerun an entire business operation merely because its notification failed after database commit. A transactional outbox can store business state and delivery intent in one local transaction, then hand delivery to a worker. That establishes responsibility, not globally exactly-once external effects. This is design analysis without an implemented messaging system or fault tests.

Two successful calls do not share a commit

Writing an order then sending email leaves a crash window between them. Reversing the order can send email before a transaction rolls back. The issue is the missing shared boundary, not insufficiently adjacent code.

The PostgreSQL 18 transaction tutorial supports grouping database operations atomically. Saving an order and delivery record together is this article’s design inference; the tutorial does not prescribe an outbox architecture or worker semantics.

Make the handoff recoverable

A record can contain a stable event ID, business object, event type or version, necessary content reference, state, and retry information. Minimize copied personal data. After commit, workers discover pending records; replacement workers need durable evidence to decide takeover after failure.

Concurrent workers require an explicit claim, lease, or locking policy, including stalled owners and retry deadlines. An in-memory sending flag cannot establish recovery after restart.

Define whether event content is a historical snapshot or a later read. If an order is canceled before notification, business meaning and ordering should determine delivery, not whichever state the worker happens to observe.

External success leaves another crash window

A worker can send successfully and fail before marking completion. A replacement then sends again. The receiver needs stable-ID deduplication where possible, or the business must accept and constrain duplicates. A local state field cannot promise global exactly-once effects across separate systems.

Ordering also needs a scope. Per-object ordering and cross-object invariants differ; a global serial queue can impose unnecessary cost. Add coordination only where required.

Proposed tests stop before commit, after commit but before send, and after external success but before acknowledgement. Include competing claims and persistent backlog. None were run. Inspect external effect counts, takeover, and manual recovery as well as database state.

A noncritical notification may justify a simpler lossy approach if explicitly accepted. For critical delivery, define event identity, unknown-outcome lookup, and backlog ownership before selecting a library. Review business idempotency with the handoff; a committed record is not end-to-end delivery.
