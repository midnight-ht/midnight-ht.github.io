preStop Is Configured but Requests Still Fail: Budget the Whole Shutdown

Budget Kubernetes termination across hooks and in-flight work with observable completion rather than fixed sleep.

M Midnight Engineering notes on development, quality, security, delivery, and operations. View Author Profile
Updated 2026-10-09 · Estimated read 6 min
Translations
zh-CN en

Article

A configured preStop hook does not prove lossless shutdown. The grace period must cover stopping intake, in-flight work, and cleanup; hook execution consumes that budget too. Sleeping waits for time, not business completion. No upgrade or drain experiment was run here.

One budget covers several actions

The Kubernetes Pod lifecycle documentation describes preStop before the normal termination signal, a default thirty-second grace period that workload configuration can change, and eventual forced termination. Sidecar details and version-specific behavior need environmental review.

If a hook sleeps for much of that window, little remains for an in-progress report and connection cleanup. Extra sleep may cover one observed routing delay without accommodating long requests, consumers, or background tasks.

Give the application an explicit shutdown state

Stop taking new work, decide the fate of accepted work, then close connections and exit. Discovery, proxies, and existing connections do not necessarily stop traffic instantly. Application rejection or queueing must align with client retries.

HTTP is not the only work. Consumers may hold messages, workers may hold leases, and batches may be partially written. Each needs a finish, return, or durable-recovery rule. Killing a process without that contract can produce omissions or duplicate effects.

Divide the budget across routing propagation, acceptable work duration, cleanup, and margin using actual evidence. Neither a documented default nor a fast average request establishes the service’s requirement. Work that cannot finish within budget needs independent recovery.

Track accepted work to an outcome

A proposed rehearsal terminates an instance under traffic and records notification, readiness change, last accepted request, completed work, and exit. Inspect user outcomes, retries, and duplicate effects. These are proposed observations, not collected logs.

Force deletion or node failure may bypass graceful handling. A successful release drain therefore does not replace durable state and idempotency for abrupt disappearance.

If fixed sleep is a temporary measure, record what delay it addresses and when it will be replaced. Without observable work state, do not report that every request drained. Add controlled in-flight or task-state visibility and inspect proxy behavior.

Keep shutdown and probes consistent: an exiting instance should not accept inappropriate work or trigger contradictory recovery. Map one real task from acceptance to completion and handoff, then review it with probe responsibilities.
