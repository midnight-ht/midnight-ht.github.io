Define error-budget restrictions and exceptions before an incident

Define error-budget restrictions, exceptions and release evidence before urgent decisions.

M Midnight Engineering notes on development, quality, security, delivery, and operations. View Author Profile
Updated 2026-10-15 · Estimated read 5 min
Translations
zh-CN en

Article

An exhausted error budget should trigger an agreed policy, not an improvised ban on every action. Define the service, measurement window, restricted changes, exceptions, and recovery criteria. Security or urgent recovery work may continue, with ownership and evidence.

A number needs a decision contract

The Google SRE Workbook example policy restricts changes after budget exhaustion over the preceding four weeks while allowing P0 and security fixes. It is an example, not a universal mandate. Preserve scope, classification, and responsibility rather than copying only the freeze sentence.

Imagine pausing non-reliability features while a defect still needs repair. A blanket ban can obstruct recovery; declaring every change urgent removes the constraint. This example was not enacted and carries no risk-reduction or business-benefit measurement.

Agree which actions add risk, which reduce current risk, and who approves exceptions. Configuration and data changes can matter alongside code; blocking only one route may leave equivalent risk elsewhere.

Keep the measurement trustworthy

Population, success definitions, and windows affect the budget. Missing real failures or counting out-of-scope activity changes the decision. Corrections need traceability rather than last-minute relabeling to permit release.

Improving metrics also do not establish that all consequences ended. Define restriction removal, required evidence, and unfinished recovery ownership. No universally optimal window or waiting period is prescribed.

Make exceptions reviewable

Bind urgent work to a version, purpose, known risks, and outcome verification. Accelerating the process is not permanent exemption from tests, ownership, or rollback planning. Omitted checks remain explicit rather than being retrospectively declared passed.

A proposed exercise covers sufficient and exhausted budgets, incorrect telemetry scope, and emergency repair. It was not run, and no incident-prevention result is claimed. See incident priorities and responsibility. The budget identifies a decision condition; policy must specify who decides what using which evidence.
