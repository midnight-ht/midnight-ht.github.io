Track image identity beyond tag names and pull-policy labels

Verify image digests and actual pull policy without treating cache reuse as stale deployment.

M Midnight Engineering notes on development, quality, security, delivery, and operations. View Author Profile
Updated 2026-10-15 · Estimated read 5 min
Translations
zh-CN en

Article

imagePullPolicy Always does not mean downloading every image layer again. The runtime resolves image identity through the registry and can reuse matching local layers. Verify the actual digest and workload rather than inferring version adoption from transfer volume.

Resolution and retransmission differ

The Kubernetes images guide describes registry resolution, digest selection, and fetching missing layers under Always. It recommends meaningful versions or digests instead of relying on latest. The checked page is marked updated in February 2026; that does not establish any particular cluster's configuration.

Imagine two image versions sharing most layers. Little transfer for the newer version does not prove stale code. Conversely, a download does not prove the approved version reached the business. No deployment, transfer, or startup measurement was performed here.

Tags are names; digests identify content. If tags can move, retain more than the tag in release records. Digest pinning also needs a maintenance and security-update process rather than freezing content without responsibility.

Defaults are not recalculated from later tag changes

The documentation says imagePullPolicy is set at initial object creation and is not automatically recalculated when tags or digests change. Switching an existing object to latest does not establish that its policy became Always. Inspect the actual object, including template and history effects.

Changing registry content under a tag likewise does not establish a workload rollout. Identify updated instances and their actual image identities. Acquisition policy is only one part of release completion.

Connect approval with runtime identity

Proposed checks compare declared versions, resolved digests, running instances, and business behavior under caches, shared layers, and historical settings. Registry unavailability also needs policy-specific validation; local layers do not guarantee every policy starts offline.

Bind runtime identity to an instance and observation time. An exited instance cannot establish the current serving version, and one record does not prove all traffic switched during rollout.

No cluster or rollback was tested. See build-artifact identity. Cache reuse can be correct; what matters is whether the reused content satisfies the current declaration.
