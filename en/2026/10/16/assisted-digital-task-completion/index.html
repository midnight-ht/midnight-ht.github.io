How can you verify a supported application actually reached submission?

Follow an application from phone support to in-person evidence collection and check the original draft, evidence and submission receipt.

M Midnight Engineering notes on development, quality, security, delivery, and operations. View Author Profile
Updated 2026-11-13 · Estimated read 14 min
Translations
zh-CN en

Article

Key takeaways

  • After the call, check the original draft.
  • Evidence collection and submission have separate states.
  • Use the receipt for the same application to verify submission.

A closed support ticket tells you that a contact ended. To verify that an application was submitted, find the submission status and receipt for that same application, then check that the applicant knows what each means. If an office has accepted the evidence but the application still shows as a draft, it still needs an explicit submission step.

Imagine a public service that asks applicants to upload proof of address. One applicant can fill in the form on a phone, but their proof exists on paper and their device cannot submit it as required. A phone agent can ask whether the applicant can obtain the evidence, which location they can use, and whether they need to keep what they already entered. Those questions establish the material, channel and draft. Sending another upload tutorial can help someone who cannot find the control. It cannot provide the missing condition in this case.

The GOV.UK introduction to assisted digital support describes barriers involving trust, confidence, internet access, skills and motivation to overcome those barriers. It calls for research into what help users need and identifies phone, in-person and sometimes webchat support. That guidance applies to UK public services. The workflow below borrows its approach to identifying barriers and coordinating support. The fields and states are design suggestions for the hypothetical service.

Carry one draft across the phone call and the office visit

Start the walkthrough with a fictional application, D17. The browser has already saved the address. The application remains a draft and lacks the required evidence. The phone agent confirms that the applicant wants to provide the material in person, explains how necessary information will be passed along and registers the handoff within the allowed scope. The office needs to know the draft, the missing item and when the handoff expires. The phone ticket need not contain the full address or the contents of the document. These are teaching states and identifiers. They have not been tested in a real service or with research participants.

At the office, staff first verify identity and access using the service’s procedures, then open D17. They check that the address is still there and that the missing item is accurate. After accepting the document, they check whether the business record lists it. If the service requires the applicant to confirm a declaration, staff provide an accessible way for the applicant to review the final contents. Only after confirmation and submission does the application system generate a receipt. The support record can close when the visit ends. Acceptance still checks the application status, receipt identifier and the applicant’s next action separately.

Use three distinct assertions in a test environment. When the call ends, the draft retains the address, the document is still missing and no business receipt exists. When the office records the material, the original draft remains identifiable, the evidence list contains an additional item and the submission state has not changed. After the final action, the record shows submitted and the applicant receives the corresponding receipt. The three observations locate failures. One completion button or final screenshot cannot easily reveal whether the system saved two unrelated applications.

Keep support metrics from hiding a failed handoff

Try a negative case. The applicant arrives with the document and the phone ticket is closed, but office staff cannot find the draft and create another one. Starting over may look faster, yet it can leave two records and no way for the applicant to know which contains the earlier address. Check whether the handoff identifier expired, whether the office staff have the required access, and whether the original draft was removed when its retention period ended. Those causes require different recovery steps. If a new draft is necessary, explain what needs to be confirmed again and handle the previous draft under the service’s rules.

If the office can only offer an appointment and cannot submit the application directly, the call can hand off to a booking. A booking confirmation does not establish submission. Have a test participant return from the booking and check whether they can resume the original draft and know whom to contact if they cannot. Someone with a suitable device who does not understand the photo requirements may benefit from guided self-service. Someone whose device cannot meet those requirements needs a way to transfer the evidence. The channel choice depends on whether it removes the obstacle and still lets the applicant understand and confirm the submission.

The GOV.UK cross-channel service standard emphasizes the combination of channels users need, participation by front-line staff in research, feedback between online and offline services, and keeping staff informed of changes. In this example, the phone and office leads can rehearse a missing document, its handoff and the receipt together, then update both sets of instructions when evidence requirements change. Fewer calls alone do not establish a smoother application. Also check whether people can find support, resume the original draft and produce business completion evidence. See researching device conditions for recruiting around the equipment people can actually use.

Rehearse a successful submission followed by a missing receipt. Suppose D17 reached submitted status but the screen closed before displaying the receipt. When the applicant calls again, the agent follows service rules to check the same application record, explain its status and point the applicant to the original receipt. Verify that recovery returns the original identifier and submission time and that no second application was created. If the call record or draft screen cannot resolve the question, hand it to someone who can inspect the business record. This case separates delivery of the notification from recording the submission and avoids making the applicant submit again just to recover a receipt.

Does the applicant start again if they withdraw permission for assistance?

In this design the applicant withdraws permission for another person to assist. The team can revoke the unused handoff and allow the applicant to access their own draft through an authorized route. The service’s record rules govern material that has already been supplied. A note on a call cannot decide its retention or deletion. Test a draft with an authorized handoff and no submitted document. After withdrawal, office staff should lose access through that handoff while the applicant can still use the draft as allowed by the service. This follow-up is a proposed state test and was not run.
