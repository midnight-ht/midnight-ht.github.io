Design assisted digital support around task completion

Research support needs and preserve informed task completion across assistance channels.

M Midnight Engineering notes on development, quality, security, delivery, and operations. View Author Profile
Updated 2026-10-16 · Estimated read 7 min
Translations
zh-CN en

Article

Assisted digital support should help a person complete the original service task and understand what they have submitted. Sending someone back to the same website helps only when the obstacle has been removed. A tutorial link sent by an agent is not evidence of task completion.

Identify the missing condition

The GOV.UK assisted digital support guide, updated in December 2025, describes barriers involving trust, confidence, internet access, digital skills and motivation to overcome difficulties alone. It calls for research into actual support needs and describes telephone, in-person and sometimes webchat support. This is guidance for UK public services. Other organizations can borrow its research approach without importing its population exceptions or service obligations.

These barriers require different responses. Explaining where an upload control sits may help someone who cannot find it. Repeating upload instructions does not provide a suitable device. Someone who doubts the service’s authenticity may need verifiable organizational information. Treating every request as a skills problem encourages teams to improve wording while overlooking access conditions.

Design the supported task

Consider a hypothetical applicant who entered an address but cannot upload supporting evidence. This design example has not been tested in a real service. An agent would establish the blocked step, explain available assistance, required materials and information recording, then obtain agreement before proceeding. At the end, the applicant should know what was saved, what remains and how to resume. Agents should not guess answers to improve completion figures.

A telephone-to-office handoff should carry only necessary, authorized task information. Privacy arrangements depend on the business context. Closing a support ticket does not establish that the application was submitted: linked records still need distinct state meanings.

Evaluate the supported journey

Observe target users under the equipment and support conditions actually available to them. Record when help becomes necessary, whether it enables progress, whether confirmation is understood and what disappears across channels. Recruiting only existing website visitors cannot establish that nobody needs assistance; people unable to try independently need suitable research methods too.

Keep requests for help, support received, task completion and later rework separate. Fewer calls could reflect improvement or an inaccessible support entrance. Establish evidence before choosing an explanation.

Support instructions also need maintenance ownership. Changes to evidence requirements, pages or identity checks should reach agents and handoff processes. Change notifications, escalation paths and sampling checks keep the service coherent after launch.

Before release, ask whether someone helped at the hardest step can identify their current status and next action. Researching device conditions provides related context. Include assistance in service acceptance, with owners for updated instructions, escalation and task-outcome checks.
