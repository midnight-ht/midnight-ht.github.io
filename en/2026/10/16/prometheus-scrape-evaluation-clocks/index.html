Separate Prometheus scraping from rule evaluation

Trace scraping, evaluation and later notification stages before changing alert timing.

M Midnight Engineering notes on development, quality, security, delivery, and operations. View Author Profile
Updated 2026-10-16 · Estimated read 6 min
Translations
zh-CN en

Article

Increasing Prometheus scrape frequency does not necessarily reduce notification delay by the same proportion. Scraping obtains samples, rule evaluation computes expressions, and later stages may include pending conditions and notification waits. Investigate these stages separately.

Two schedules have different jobs

The Prometheus configuration reference defines scrape_interval and evaluation_interval separately for scraping and rule evaluation, with relevant scoped settings. This describes the current reference, not the actual values in a reader’s system or a guarantee across versions.

Imagine an abnormal sample already stored while its rule awaits another evaluation. Storage alone does not complete alert processing. Conversely, faster evaluation cannot create fresh data that has not been scraped. This timing scenario was not executed or benchmarked.

Trace event to notification

Record the business event, metric update, sample acquisition, rule observation and eventual notification where applicable. Real deployments differ. Identify each timestamp’s clock and observation point rather than conflating event, collection and display times.

If a rule has a duration condition, inspect when it begins, resets and interacts with later delivery. This article does not document every alert state machine or notification product; those need system-specific reading. Changing one scrape parameter cannot establish a fixed end-to-end delay.

Metrics may themselves update later than the event. Exporter availability and data arrival also affect what evaluation sees. Adding configured intervals arithmetically does not prove an operational upper bound. Observe which waits are expected and which arise from failures or missing data.

Frequency needs a purpose

More frequent collection and computation change workload, but the impact requires measurement. None was measured here, and no universal best interval is proposed. Whether brief spikes merit intervention depends on business risk and metric semantics. Faster detection can still be noisy or unactionable.

Define the event to detect and what responders can do before choosing granularity. Reducing delay on an unusable notification may add little value. High-risk omissions require coverage and failure-path review, not merely a smaller number in one setting.

Proposed acceptance work creates a safe, recognizable condition change, records stage times, compares configuration with behavior and checks visibility when scraping or evaluation fails. These checks were not executed; no alert-latency commitment is claimed.

Prometheus missing-data signals discusses an adjacent problem. Report what the actual chain demonstrated and which stages remain uncertain. Managing acquisition, evaluation and delivery separately makes latency work actionable.
