Separate concepts in double-barreled survey questions

Separate survey concepts and preserve the context needed to interpret answers.

M Midnight Engineering notes on development, quality, security, delivery, and operations. View Author Profile
Updated 2026-10-16 · Estimated read 7 min
Translations
zh-CN en

Article

A rating for both speed and quality usually cannot identify which experience a respondent is evaluating. Separate the concepts needed for a decision, then decide whether an additional overall question has a purpose. Splitting questions should improve interpretation rather than merely lengthen a survey.

Experiences can move in opposite directions

Pew Research Center’s question-writing guidance explains that double-barreled questions ask people to evaluate multiple concepts, making answers difficult to provide and interpret. It recommends asking one question at a time and considering concrete language, terminology and respondents’ understanding. This is methodological guidance, not an experiment conducted for this article or evidence of a particular improvement percentage.

Imagine asking whether someone is satisfied with customer support’s response speed and resolution quality. A middle rating might mean a fast but useless reply, or a slow but effective resolution. Combining these respondents obscures the appropriate action. More responses cannot recover a distinction the question never captured.

Start with the decision

A staffing decision may concern the first response, handling progress or final resolution. Evaluating useful advice requires a different focus. Some evidence may come from case records; a survey need not measure everything.

One possible revision asks separately about the first response and the state of resolution. Options still need research. Respondents may never have contacted support, may not remember, or may have an open case. Give these situations appropriate paths rather than forcing a rating.

An overall experience question can remain when it serves a clear purpose, but it answers a different question. It does not replace diagnosis. Equally, averaging component scores does not automatically measure overall satisfaction. Define aggregation rules and intended use before examining the results.

Check how people interpret the question

Before launch, ask target respondents to explain what the wording means, which experience they recall and why they choose an option. This is a proposed research activity, not one performed here. If some interpret speed as first response and others as final resolution, deleting a conjunction is insufficient: the event and time boundary need clarification.

Check burden and ordering too. Repetitive subdivisions can cause fatigue, especially for people without relevant experience. Retain questions that change an action, explain a result or test a consequential assumption, and verify routing.

Save the exact wording, response options, audience and survey version with the analysis. Old combined ratings are not automatically comparable with newly separated ratings. When wording and recruitment also change, a score difference cannot simply be attributed to service improvement. State when old answers cannot be decomposed.

A practical test is whether a low response tells the team what to change. If two opposing explanations remain, revisit the concepts. A purpose for each form question applies the same discipline to information collection.
