When Address Lookup Fails: Carry Manual Entry Through Save and Review

Separate database misses from service-area rejection, choose fields by downstream use, and verify manual entry through save and review.

M Midnight Engineering notes on development, quality, security, delivery, and operations. View Author Profile
Updated 2026-10-21 · Estimated read 16 min
Translations
zh-CN en

Article

Key takeaways

  • A database miss, lookup outage, and service-area rejection require different messages.
  • Manual field structure should follow country range and downstream need for accurate subfields.
  • Manual entry must reach the save API, review page, and operational processing.

A new apartment building opens, but an applicant cannot find its real address in the lookup list. If the form only accepts a selected result, the provider's possibly outdated database has quietly become the service's eligibility rule. No result can mean at least three different things: the person mistyped the address, the database has not listed it, or the service does not accept that location. They need different responses. The GOV.UK Design System address pattern recommends a manual option for international addresses and for addresses missing or incorrectly listed in an address lookup. That is a useful service-design principle, not a claim that UK postcode or UPRN rules apply in every country.

Separate lookup failure from business rejection

Imagine a delivery service that operates only in City A. A customer enters “18 New Town Road, City A,” but the lookup has not yet recorded the new building. The interface should let them enter it manually and retain the status “not matched by the address database”; it should not declare the location unsupported. Another customer enters an address in City B. Even if the database finds it, the service may still be unable to deliver there. Lookup tells us what the database knows. Business rules decide service coverage. A single red “Invalid address” error gives the customer and support team no way to distinguish spelling help, a missing record, and a regional restriction.

The interface can model four distinct outcomes: selectable addresses found; no match, with manual entry available; lookup temporarily unavailable; and location outside the service area. No match is not the same as an outage. The former may invite another spelling or manual entry; the latter needs a retry path rather than repeated editing of a valid address. A blank or clearly incomplete input deserves its own message. When a provider times out, do not silently count it as a zero-result search, or the team will attribute an infrastructure problem to customers' homes. Analytics should keep these states separate if the team wants to know whether manual entry catches coverage gaps.

Place the manual-entry route where people still understand the lookup task—for example, beneath the candidate list as “Can't find your address? Enter it manually.” Requiring three failed searches before showing it makes access depend on whether the database happens to return a plausible result. A service expecting local and international addresses may show the choice before search. The GOV.UK pattern addresses UK public services, so exact copy, layout, and regional rules need testing against the product's own tasks. The portable principle is that lookup coverage should not determine who can submit a legitimate address.

Choose manual fields by downstream use

Manual entry is not automatically a fixed set of five boxes. If a service only needs a deliverable address for a paper notice, a multiline field that supports paste may be enough, alongside any country or region selection the business actually needs. Show the original text on the review page. If the service must assign an agent by district, calculate charges, or integrate with another structured system, it needs dependable subfields. The GOV.UK guidance reserves multiple text inputs for cases where the countries are known and one format supports them; a textarea suits varied formats when precise street or postcode components are not required. A database column named province is not a reason to force a customer in a country without that administrative level to invent one.

Suppose a cross-border application covers Country A, where province and postcode are customary, and Country B, where those fields do not map cleanly. Making both mandatory excludes valid addresses from B. Storing everything in one free-text blob may, on the other hand, fail a real service-area or tax calculation. A more deliberate contract records country explicitly, then selects subfields according to the target country and actual business use. It can also retain the full delivery text the customer entered. An automatic parser's output should be marked as inferred or awaiting confirmation, rather than silently replacing the original. A parsing failure is not proof that the address does not exist.

Switching from lookup to manual entry must preserve a draft. If a person already entered postcode AB1 2CD, it can be prefilled into an appropriate field, but a street guessed by the provider is not yet customer-confirmed. If the person switches back, keep their manual draft long enough to compare and choose, or say precisely which fields will be replaced. On review, distinguish “selected and confirmed lookup result” from “customer-entered address awaiting verification.” That is provenance, not a judgment that manual-entry customers are less trustworthy. If staff must verify an address later, tell the user about the next step and notification path. A success page should not pretend that lookup or manual submission has already completed address verification.

Carry the route through the API and operations

A common failure is adding a manual form while the save API still requires a nonempty addressId. The customer fills every field and presses Continue, only to receive “Select an address” from the server. The exit was decorative. The API needs a way to represent two input branches: a selected provider identifier with its display value, or customer-entered fields with provenance such as source=manual. Validate the mandatory fields for the chosen branch. Do not hide “must match the provider database” in a shared validator that also runs on manual addresses. A genuine service-area restriction belongs in an independent business rule with a specific response.

For example, an applicant manually enters “18 New Town Road, Building 2, Unit 501,” chooses City A, and saves. The review page shows that address; they correct the unit to 502 before final submission. The final record must contain the corrected value, and the support interface should display the same version. If the back office sees only addressId=null without the entered text, staff cannot send the notice and the task still fails. Trace input, request payload, persisted value, review page, staff view, and subsequent notification. Record source and verification status separately. Address data is sensitive; logs should keep necessary error codes and trace IDs rather than scattering full home addresses for debugging.

Further negative cases include normalization that deletes the building number, or a refreshed review page that returns to an empty lookup. Both reveal that the provider's format is still treated as the only source of truth. Matching can make entry easier and automate some later work, but the customer's original text, manual corrections, and verification results need an audit trail. If staff later associate a manual address with a canonical identifier, preserve the submitted text and the reason for the association. “Linked afterward” should not be rewritten as “the customer selected this result.”

An acceptance set can use four contrasting examples: a known address inside the area, an unknown address inside the area, a known address outside the area, and a timed-out lookup. Follow each through review, save, staff processing, and customer feedback, not just the suggestion menu. Also test switching routes, long addresses, countries without postcodes, corrections, and repeated submissions. If the business truly cannot handle a region, explain the restriction and offer an appropriate completion or support route; the related article on assisted channels completing a task discusses handoff. This article did not operate a live address provider or application. The test of a manual fallback is whether a real but unlisted address reaches final processing with clear provenance—not whether the page contains the words “manual entry.”
