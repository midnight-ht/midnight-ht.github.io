A successful click does not settle target size and spacing

Check the active target and nearby controls before treating one successful click as evidence of adequate target size.

M Midnight Engineering notes on development, quality, security, delivery, and operations. View Author Profile
Updated 2026-10-19 · Estimated read 7 min
Translations
zh-CN en

Article

Clicking a small delete icon once does not establish that its target meets WCAG requirements. It establishes that one attempt succeeded. A size review still needs the active region, the position of neighbouring targets, and the conditions of any exception. The visible icon and the region accepting pointer input may have different boundaries.

Measure the active region

WCAG 2.2 Understanding Target Size (Minimum) explains Success Criterion 2.5.8 at Level AA. Pointer targets generally need to be at least 24 by 24 CSS pixels, with exceptions for spacing, equivalent controls, inline content, user agent controls, and essential presentation. This is a specific criterion, rather than a requirement to draw every icon at that size or evidence of complete accessibility conformance.

Consider a small close symbol inside a larger button. If the surrounding padding accepts the same action, review that active area. Adding an outer margin merely moves the symbol away from neighbours; it does not enlarge the target when the margin cannot be clicked. This is a hypothetical design example, without browser measurements. A real review must inspect hit regions and overlaps.

Rounded or irregular targets also require care. To satisfy the size route directly, the target must contain an axis-aligned 24-pixel square. An outer bounding box with sufficient width and height can include corners that do not accept input. Recording only those two dimensions can miss that distinction.

Spacing is a geometric exception

For an undersized target, the explanation describes a 24-CSS-pixel-diameter circle centred on its bounding box. That circle must not intersect another target or the corresponding circle of another undersized target. Look above and below the control as well as along its row.

Suppose two small icons have a visible gap, but one sits beside a whole clickable card. Its circle could still intersect the card target. Increasing the gap between the two icons might leave that problem unresolved. Conversely, a short target with no nearby targets may meet the spacing exception. A gap from one example should therefore not become a universal layout rule.

A successful click and satisfaction of the criterion belong in different records: an observation in one case, geometry and applicable conditions in the other. Even after the minimum is met, task observation can reveal whether a consequential action remains difficult to activate accurately. The minimum does not promise comfortable use for everyone.

Explain the exception being used

An inline exception needs an actual sentence context or a size constrained by the line height of non-target text. Text in a toolbar does not automatically qualify. An equivalent-control exception needs an identified control on the same page that performs the same function. Essential presentation needs a reason tied to the information, such as a map, rather than a preference for density.

A useful handover identifies target boundaries, neighbours, the chosen size or exception route, and dynamic states such as expanded menus. When enlarging active areas, check that adjacent actions do not compete for overlapping space. This article does not perform a conformance assessment. Zoom and reflow addresses another layout boundary. Draw the active regions first; then choose larger targets, more space, or a justified exception.
