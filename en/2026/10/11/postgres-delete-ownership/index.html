Choose cascading deletion from ownership, not convenience

Choose referential deletion from lifetimes, shared references and recovery requirements.

M Midnight Engineering notes on development, quality, security, delivery, and operations. View Author Profile
Updated 2026-10-11 · Estimated read 6 min
Translations
zh-CN en

Article

A foreign key protects references but does not decide the business scope of deletion. Choose cascading behavior from shared lifetimes, audit needs, recovery and ownership rather than the convenience of one statement.

References do not prove ownership

Imagine an order with exclusively owned temporary lines and a product shared by other orders. Deleting an order and deleting a product express different intentions. Treating every relationship as dependency can remove data still needed elsewhere. This is a design example, not a reported production incident.

The PostgreSQL 18 constraints documentation discusses ON DELETE CASCADE through components versus independent objects. The database executes the chosen referential action; the application still decides whether it expresses the intended lifecycle. A foreign key alone cannot establish that one object is merely a component.

Define what deletion means

During model review, distinguish removal from business visibility and physical removal. Identify associations that lose their purpose with the parent, then establish the state needed after an accidental deletion. Reports, audits and other workflows may depend on retained records. Conversely, retaining everything introduces filtering and cleanup responsibilities.

Soft deletion normally changes a marker rather than triggering a physical-delete referential action. Define whether children become inactive, how queries exclude inactive parents and who performs final cleanup. These are application rules. Local constraints cannot coordinate cross-service deletion by themselves.

Inspect the relationship graph

Follow multiple levels of references, keeping shared objects out of exclusively owned branches. Assess the execution and resource consequences of large deletions using representative data in a recoverable environment. No migration or deletion experiment was executed here; no lock-duration or performance claim follows.

A blocked deletion can be the intended protection for independently referenced data. Explain the dependency and a resolution path instead of automatically clearing every reference after an error. Administrative permissions should not silently widen user intent.

Start with one entity and document owned components, shared references, historical dependencies and recovery strategy before selecting a database action. The savepoint discussion separates local recovery from overall commitment. Clear ownership helps maintainers assess risk more effectively than minimizing statement count.
