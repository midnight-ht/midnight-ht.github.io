Plural rules select categories, not translated sentences

Select complete localized messages, keeping cardinal, ordinal, zero-state and unknown-count semantics distinct.

M Midnight Engineering notes on development, quality, security, delivery, and operations. View Author Profile
Updated 2026-10-11 · Estimated read 6 min
Translations
zh-CN en

Article

Plural localization starts by selecting a category for a locale and quantity, then choosing a complete message. Adding an English “s” handles only some English cases. It cannot cover other languages, ordinals, word order or context.

Equal quantities do not imply equal structures

The MDN Intl.PluralRules documentation describes locale- and option-dependent category selection. The result is a message-selection category, not a translated sentence. Its English, Chinese and Arabic examples illustrate different cardinal categories. Verify actual target locales and options rather than generalizing one language’s rules.

Imagine displaying remaining tasks. English may distinguish one from several, Chinese can often use one structure, and other languages need more branches. This hypothetical example reports no language-coverage test. Describing a task’s ordinal position is a different problem and should not reuse cardinal selection blindly.

Translate the whole message

Provide complete templates instead of concatenating number, verb and noun fragments. Fragment reuse prevents translators from adjusting word order and obscures grammatical relationships. Format numbers for the relevant locale separately; category choice and number display serve different purposes.

Zero also has product meaning. An empty workload may deserve “There are no tasks requiring attention” rather than a mechanical counting template. A locale without a distinct zero category can still need a dedicated empty state. Unknown quantities must not appear as zero: loading differs from confirmed absence.

Preserve meaning during fallback

Define fallback for a missing category and expose the gap during development. Silently borrowing singular text can produce incorrect language and conceal unfinished translation. Validate message identity, locale, required categories and fallback policy, not merely equal key counts. Having a fallback does not establish translation quality.

Proposed data covers zero, one, several, applicable fractions and separate cardinal and ordinal scenarios. Someone familiar with the language should assess the complete page. No such checks were executed here; this article claims no universal locale support. Automation can detect missing categories but cannot confirm natural contextual expression.

Like form feedback, localization should clarify actions and state. Start with one real quantity message in full context, establish target-language templates and test data, then assess reuse and maintenance costs.
