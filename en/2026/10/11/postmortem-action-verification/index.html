An incident action closes when its risk reduction is checked

Tie incident actions to failure paths and falsifiable acceptance, without equating closed tasks with quantified risk reduction.

M Midnight Engineering notes on development, quality, security, delivery, and operations. View Author Profile
Updated 2026-10-11 · Estimated read 6 min
Translations
zh-CN en

Article

An incident action should connect a failure path, the system condition being changed and acceptance evidence. A closed task proves only that work was marked complete. Check whether the old trigger is blocked, detection is earlier or recovery is more dependable.

A verb is not an acceptance condition

The Google SRE postmortem chapter discusses blameless learning, system and process improvements, and review of impact, causes and action plans. The acceptance design here is editorial advice, not a claim about Google’s internal task standard. No real incident materials or drills were used.

Imagine an action saying “add monitoring” being closed after a chart is created, although the original problem was an unnoticed dependency timeout. A chart does not establish timely actionable notification. This hypothetical example shows why actions need a named failure path rather than a broad executable verb.

Choose the condition to change

Prevention, detection, mitigation and recovery need different evidence. Prevention examines the original trigger; detection checks signals and response; recovery checks resources, permissions and steps. Updated documentation is not verified recovery, and faster detection is not prevention.

Record ownership, dependencies, acceptance conditions and remaining risk, including environment and limits. Ownership coordinates work rather than assigns personal blame. Blameless discussion still needs facts and responsible task progression. State accepted-risk periods and decision conditions instead of hiding unresolved exposure in an unfinished list.

Verification must be able to reject completion

Code or configuration existence alone says little about the old failure path. Controlled faults, suitable input replay or dependency-failure checks may help, depending on impact and authorization. This article does not recommend unconditional production fault injection or present a planned drill as successful.

Detection changes also need label, routing, silencing and responder-comprehension checks. The alert-identity article illustrates present configuration without sustained activation. Process changes can use scenario checks demonstrating executability under constraints rather than merely recording that people read instructions.

Revisit evidence when permissions, migrations or dependency upgrades invalidate assumptions. Associate actions with maintainable checks or explicit review events. Without suitable observations, state only what acceptance established, not an invented percentage reduction in incident probability.

For one existing action, add its failure path, observable change and falsifiable acceptance condition before closing it. All examples here are design proposals; no tests or quantified risk reduction were produced. Separate task state from risk evidence to make improvement inspectable.
