A completed click is not a completed business operation

Wait for rereadable business outcomes rather than treating a completed click or transient notice as durable success.

M Midnight Engineering notes on development, quality, security, delivery, and operations. View Author Profile
Updated 2026-10-11 · Estimated read 5 min
Translations
zh-CN en

Article

After a click completes, wait for a condition representing the business outcome before asserting it. Clickability and completed submission are different states. Reading transient text once can mistake ordinary asynchronous updates for failure.

Actions and outcomes have separate waits

The Playwright assertions documentation distinguishes auto-retrying web assertions from ordinary value assertions and requires awaiting asynchronous retrying assertions. Web assertions recheck within a timeout; ordinary assertions do not automatically reread the page. No browser cases were executed here, so no pass-rate or stability improvement is claimed.

Imagine clicking Save while server processing and rendering continue. Old text does not establish final failure. Conversely, a disabled button may indicate only request initiation. Choose a completion signal from the product contract rather than whichever transition is easiest to observe.

The observed object matters

A string captured before an assertion stays unchanged when the page updates. A proposed retry should observe something that can be located or read again and genuinely represents the result. If persistent state is not visible, an authorized backend check may be appropriate as an explicit test design, not an unexplained bypass of the user journey.

Fixed delays postpone observation without naming the event. Short delays risk false failures; long delays conceal faults longer. Condition waits still require realistic budgets grounded in the environment and service objectives. Continually increasing timeouts to eliminate failures can hide performance or reliability problems.

Stable signals must expose failure

A temporary success notice may precede evidence that nothing persisted. When the promise is durable change, separate immediate feedback from subsequent retrieval and describe what each proves. Asynchronous jobs may acknowledge acceptance rather than completion.

Proposed cases include slow responses, rejected submissions, delayed updates and revisiting the page. Verify that locators identify this operation's result: one of several matching notices is insufficient. These cases were not run and no successful logs are presented. Tool retries cannot establish that an assertion checks the right thing.

The HTTP 202 contract clarifies acceptance versus completion. For one flaky case, name the business event being awaited, then verify rereading, failure detection and the stopping condition.
