Tail sampling cannot recover traces already discarded upstream

Tail sampling selects received traces; upstream drops, routing and decision windows still constrain error coverage.

M Midnight Engineering notes on development, quality, security, delivery, and operations. View Author Profile
Updated 2026-10-10 · Estimated read 5 min
Translations
zh-CN en

Article

Tail sampling cannot recover data discarded and never sent upstream. It can select more useful received traces, but coverage still depends on head sampling, transport, routing and decision conditions. No Collector configuration or storage-cost measurement was performed here.

Decision location limits available information

OpenTelemetry’s sampling concepts distinguish early head decisions from tail decisions using all or most spans. Tail policies can consider errors, overall latency and attributes, while requiring state and resources. Both approaches can be combined; actual SDK and Collector configuration needs version-specific review.

If an entry-point policy drops a trace before a child fails, a later “retain errors” rule cannot reconstruct missing spans. Claims of retaining every error therefore need an explicit reception scope and conditions.

Lower volume does not ensure equally useful evidence across high-traffic endpoints, rare jobs and new releases. Tie policies to investigative needs, not merely data-reduction targets. No cost saving or detection rate was measured.

Completeness has operational costs

Tail decisions retain state while information arrives. Trace duration, arrival order and processing distribution affect visibility. Short windows may miss late spans; long windows increase retention responsibility. Consult the actual component and verify capacity rather than copying a universal value.

Ensure fragments of a trace reach compatible aggregation state. More nodes do not automatically improve completeness. Restarts, buffering pressure and transport failures require monitoring of the telemetry pipeline itself.

Policy attributes also need provenance and privacy boundaries. Low-sensitivity classifications may support investigation without propagating customer email, as discussed in context propagation boundaries. Confirm required attributes appear consistently on relevant spans.

Verify selection and its exclusions

Proposed tests cover normal, failing, long-running and late-arriving requests plus collector restarts. Include upstream-discarded traces to demonstrate the exclusion explicitly. These checks remain unexecuted.

Selected traces alone do not establish total business error counts; selection can be biased. Combine suitable aggregate metrics and logs, and assess storage, transport and sampling computation together. Record reception scope, policy, capacity and unknown gaps so missing evidence can be explained.
