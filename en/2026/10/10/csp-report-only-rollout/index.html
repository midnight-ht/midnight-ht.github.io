No CSP reports does not prove an enforcement policy is ready

Verify reporting and workflow coverage before enforcing CSP; an empty report stream is not proof of safety.

M Midnight Engineering notes on development, quality, security, delivery, and operations. View Author Profile
Updated 2026-10-10 · Estimated read 6 min
Translations
zh-CN en

Article

An empty CSP report stream does not prove enforcement is safe. It may mean no violations, broken collection or missing workflow coverage. Verify reporting and coverage before the enforcing policy. This hypothetical administration rollout changes no headers on this site and includes no executed security tests.

Reporting and enforcement differ

MDN’s CSP guide distinguishes enforcing and report-only policies. Report-only observes without enforcing that policy, and both can coexist. Browser support still varies by directive and version; a configuration file is not evidence of delivered headers.

A homepage visit does not exercise login redirects, report exports or a dynamically loaded editor. Inventory actual workflows and resource dependencies, inspecting headers in real browsers. Proxies, caches and routes can deliver different policies.

The receiver should demonstrate a predictable controlled test report. Otherwise absence cannot distinguish safety from collection failure. Arrange tests safely rather than injecting dangerous content into user pages. Reports may contain sensitive URLs or untrusted input, requiring controlled storage and access.

Do not turn every report into permission

Distinguish legitimate dependencies, browser extensions and untrusted input before changing code or policy. Adding every reported domain expands trust indiscriminately. A resource needed on one route need not receive universal permission; maintain ownership for scoped policies.

Dynamic-script authorization must match framework, caching and generation behavior. For nonce-based authorization, a constant string is not a per-response random value, and broad allowances should not become permanent merely to suppress errors. No copy-and-run policy is supplied here.

Enforce within an explicit page or traffic scope and retain a known rollback configuration. Removing every protection is not a controlled rollback. Monitor user tasks as well as reports: broken login or editing is different from a low report count.

Capture actual behavior

Proposed acceptance evidence includes browser/application versions, delivered headers, controlled allowed and blocked resources, essential workflows and rollback outcomes. Preserve report-only and enforcing evidence separately. Extension reports do not automatically justify weaker policies or establish attack success.

These checks were not executed. CSP complements input handling, output encoding and failure-path quality gates. Verify collection first, then actual tasks under enforcement.
