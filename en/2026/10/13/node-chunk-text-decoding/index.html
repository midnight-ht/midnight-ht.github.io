Decode split text characters without confusing them with message frames

Preserve split-character decoding state, then apply independent framing and end-of-input rules.

M Midnight Engineering notes on development, quality, security, delivery, and operations. View Author Profile
Updated 2026-10-13 · Estimated read 6 min
Translations
zh-CN en

Article

A network byte chunk need not contain complete characters or complete business messages. Use a stateful decoder for multibyte text, then handle message framing separately. Independent chunk-to-string conversion can produce replacement characters at split boundaries; correct decoding does not assemble messages automatically.

Byte boundaries are not character boundaries

The Node.js StringDecoder documentation explains buffering incomplete multibyte characters until subsequent input completes them. At the end, remaining bytes that cannot form valid characters may produce replacement characters under the encoding rules. This is the checked version-24 branch, not a promise to recover arbitrary damaged input.

Imagine Chinese text whose character bytes span two reads. Decoding each read independently cannot use the next chunk to complete the first. A stateful decoder delays the incomplete portion. This assumes continuous bytes split into arbitrary chunks; it cannot reconstruct bytes that were lost. The example explains the mechanism and was not executed as a network experiment.

The protocol must establish the encoding. A decoder does not infer it reliably because the content appears Chinese. If an encoding is required, define how violations are handled rather than cycling through encodings until the text looks plausible.

Decoded text still needs framing

A text protocol may delimit messages by lines, lengths, or another rule. One chunk can contain part of a message or several messages. For hypothetical line records, retain the unfinished line after decoding and pass completed records to the parser. Treating every decoder output as a record makes business behavior depend on transport chunking.

Bound the message buffer. A peer that never sends a delimiter must not create unlimited retention. The protocol determines whether an unterminated final record is valid when the connection closes. Stream completion does not establish record completeness.

Give state an input lifetime

Separate connections and independent inputs need separate decoding and framing state. Reusing unfinished state can mix the tail of one input into another. Define cleanup after reconnection, cancellation, and failure. Replacement characters are not evidence of lossless recovery.

A proposed test plan splits characters at different byte positions, combines records in one chunk, ends with incomplete characters, and sends oversized unfinished records. Check decoded text and parsed messages separately rather than merely absence of exceptions. These remain proposed tests, not compatibility results. For the output-side boundary, see writable-stream backpressure.
