Password recovery needs an explicit session-revocation policy

Define session and recovery-channel handling beyond acceptance of a new password.

M Midnight Engineering notes on development, quality, security, delivery, and operations. View Author Profile
Updated 2026-10-13 · Estimated read 6 min
Translations
zh-CN en

Article

Changing a password does not necessarily invalidate existing sessions. Recovery needs an explicit policy for old sessions, unused recovery links, and recovery channels. After suspected compromise, also examine whether an attacker changed those channels. Acceptance of a new password alone does not establish restored account control.

Forgetting a password and recovering from compromise differ

The OWASP Forgot Password Cheat Sheet discusses post-reset session handling and warns that an attacker may retain sessions or modify recovery information. For recovery after suspected compromise, it recommends invalidating existing sessions and outstanding reset or recovery links. This is general design guidance, not diagnosis or revocation performed on a real account.

Imagine a user resetting a password after suspicious activity while another device retains access. Password changes and session validation may not be automatically coupled. Testing only whether the new password works omits the old session’s access. This hypothetical example does not imply every product behaves that way.

Ordinary recovery also needs a clear session policy, potentially allowing a user choice or automatic invalidation according to security requirements. Suspected compromise calls for stronger control recovery. Explain the user experience, affected sessions, and how server-side enforcement is confirmed.

Recovery channels can preserve access

Unauthorized changes to email, phone, multifactor authentication, or other recovery methods can remain significant after a password change. Define how trustworthy recovery methods are checked and restored instead of repeatedly using a potentially compromised route.

Support staff must preserve identity-verification and privacy boundaries rather than accepting an unsupported ownership assertion. There is no universal verification checklist here; risk, available evidence, and support capability shape the product’s process.

Include revocation in acceptance

A proposed review separately covers the new credential, old sessions, unused links, and recovery configuration. Confirm server rejection rather than a logged-out screen alone, and cover devices or interfaces with different validation paths. Failed revocation needs an investigable state and owner.

Record recovery and revocation outcomes without logging secret tokens. These checks were not executed, and no individual product is certified safe. For the broader credential lifecycle, see rotation and old-credential revocation; account recovery additionally requires identity and recovery-channel decisions.
