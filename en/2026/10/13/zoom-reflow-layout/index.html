Test zoom reflow separately from a narrow mobile viewport

Verify information and operations after zoom reflow, keeping two-dimensional exceptions local.

M Midnight Engineering notes on development, quality, security, delivery, and operations. View Author Profile
Updated 2026-10-13 · Estimated read 6 min
Translations
zh-CN en

Article

A usable mobile layout does not independently establish usability after desktop zoom. Reflow checks must preserve information and operations, not merely remove horizontal scrollbars. Hiding essential content may improve a screenshot while preventing the user’s task.

Narrow windows and zoom need separate evidence

The WCAG 2.2 Understanding Reflow document describes preserving information and functionality at specified equivalent dimensions without requiring two-dimensional scrolling. For vertically read content, the width is 320 CSS pixels; 400% zoom from 1280 CSS pixels is the documented equivalence. This explains one criterion, not complete accessibility certification for this site or a hypothetical page.

Mobile and zoomed desktop layouts may encounter different conditions. Breakpoints are only part of the behavior: fixed heights, positioning, sticky regions, and expanded text also matter. Imagine an order page using cards on mobile while a fixed footer obscures its submit button after desktop zoom. A mobile screenshot cannot establish that operation is accessible.

Use realistic text and errors in proposed checks. Short labels and empty forms can miss long addresses, amounts, help, and validation messages. No browser zoom tests on these hypothetical pages were performed for this article.

Limit two-dimensional exceptions to their purpose

The document provides exceptions for content that requires a two-dimensional layout for use or meaning, such as maps or complex data tables. Having a table does not exempt the entire page. Evaluate navigation, explanations, and surrounding operations separately rather than preserving everything for implementation convenience.

Where local horizontal scrolling is necessary, make its scope understandable and avoid trapping interaction between page and inner region. Shrinking all text to retain a table is not a substitute for readable content. Actual assistive-technology and device behavior still requires validation; general design advice is not a universal guarantee.

Record task completeness

A proposed review covers finding information, entering data, reading errors, submitting, and returning, with the viewport and zoom conditions recorded. Identify the information lost or operation blocked. “No overflow” alone misses both.

After repair, check existing layout conditions as well. Complete accessibility assessment includes other criteria and contexts; one reflow check cannot establish all compliance. For preserving meaningful form feedback and business decisions, see form validation and server authority.
