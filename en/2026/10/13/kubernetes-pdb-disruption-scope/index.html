Use a PodDisruptionBudget within its disruption scope

Review PDB disruption scope separately from rollouts, fault domains and service capacity.

M Midnight Engineering notes on development, quality, security, delivery, and operations. View Author Profile
Updated 2026-10-13 · Estimated read 6 min
Translations
zh-CN en

Article

A PodDisruptionBudget constrains particular voluntary-disruption paths. It is not insurance against every unavailable replica. Node failures can still occur, and workload-managed rolling updates are not directly constrained by the PDB. Fault domains, update strategy, and capacity need separate design.

The budget has a defined scope

The Kubernetes disruptions documentation says involuntary disruptions cannot be prevented by PDBs, although they count against the budget. Unavailability during rolling updates also counts, but Deployment and StatefulSet updates are governed by workload settings rather than limited by the PDB. Counting an outcome is not the same as preventing its cause.

Imagine planned eviction from one node while another node has already failed. Existing unavailability can affect the permitted maintenance action. The PDB constrains applicable voluntary eviction; it cannot restore the failed node. This mechanism example was not executed on a cluster.

The maintenance review should also confirm which replicas the budget selector actually covers and whether the action targets the intended workload. Configuration presence and correct protected-object selection are separate evidence; omissions or unintended matches need examination before maintenance.

Identify the actor and interruption path. Tool API usage, direct deletion, and controller updates should not be treated identically merely because each can remove a Pod. Check actual versions and tool behavior rather than inferring universal protection from configuration alone.

Available replicas do not establish adequate service capacity

Remaining replicas need evidence that they can handle traffic. Pod status is one layer; the service also depends on networking, storage, upstream systems, and meaningful readiness. A conservative-looking count does not fix shared fault domains or an inaccurate readiness signal.

Review workload update limits alongside maintenance, existing failure, and capacity requirements. This is a proposed review, not an executed rollout. Configured PDBs cannot justify a zero-downtime claim without further evidence.

Investigate blocked maintenance

A proposed validation covers ordinary maintenance, existing unavailability, and rolling updates, recording which actions are limited and why. When maintenance cannot proceed, inspect selectors, availability, and workload state rather than silently removing protection. Exceptions need explicit risk and recovery ownership.

Record versions, tools, and scenarios; unrun checks remain pending. PDBs support a defined disruption boundary, not complete high availability. Connection and active-work termination need separate checks; see application and platform termination budgets.
