Assign incident command before parallel fixes collide

Coordinate incident roles, active changes and accepted handovers with explicit evidence limits.

M Midnight Engineering notes on development, quality, security, delivery, and operations. View Author Profile
Updated 2026-10-13 · Estimated read 5 min
Translations
zh-CN en

Article

During an incident, assign responsibility for priorities, system changes, status updates, and handover. The roles need not belong to four separate people, but responsibility cannot disappear because everyone is busy. Parallel work needs coordinated goals, changes, and feedback.

Command and operations answer different questions

The Google SRE incident-management chapter distinguishes command, operations, communications, and planning. It emphasizes an operations team responsible for system changes and command maintaining priorities and the overall view. This is historical practice from the book, not current news or an incident handled by this site.

Imagine one group rolling back a release, another editing configuration, and another restarting instances. Without a shared record and priority, recovery may become hard to attribute, and changes can conflict. This hypothetical example concerns coordination, not inadequate effort or operations actually performed here.

Command need not execute every action, but must know impact, working hypotheses, active changes, and the next decision. Operations must report actions and results. “Someone is handling it” does not tell colleagues whether to gather evidence, wait, or pursue an alternative.

Small teams still need explicit combined roles

One person may cover several responsibilities, but identify what lacks a separate owner. An operator handling communications may be unavailable for updates during complex work. Decide how ownership transfers before incoming questions overwhelm the arrangement.

Updates should distinguish observed impact, suspected causes, and planned actions. A hypothesis is not a confirmed root cause, and improving signals do not prove every user path has recovered. This article discusses communication structure; it sends no external messages and provides no actual incident notice.

Transfer unfinished work, not just conclusions

A handover should include active operations, pending results, evidence locations, and next-decision conditions. The incoming owner must explicitly accept responsibility. Another name appearing in a conversation does not establish a transfer.

Recovery still needs user-path and runtime evidence. An alert clearing establishes only the signal’s change. A proposed exercise can examine combined roles, handover, and conflicting changes, but none was performed and no response-time improvement is claimed. For verifiable follow-through after an incident, see postmortem action acceptance.
