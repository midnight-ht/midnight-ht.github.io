A successful HEAD response does not verify the GET body

Verify GET content under the reader’s access conditions beyond successful HEAD metadata.

M Midnight Engineering notes on development, quality, security, delivery, and operations. View Author Profile
Updated 2026-10-13 · Estimated read 6 min
Translations
zh-CN en

Article

A successful HEAD request establishes the result of that metadata request. It does not establish that the GET body was delivered completely or correctly. Verifying a public article, interface response, or download still requires body evidence under the relevant access conditions.

HEAD deliberately omits content

RFC 9110 section 9.3.2 gives HEAD the semantics of GET but prohibits response content. The server should generally send the same headers, while it may omit fields determined only during body generation. Do not turn that recommendation into an absolute requirement that every field match, or treat every missing field as a service failure.

This supports obtaining resource information without transferring a body. It does not justify claiming that all new articles are published because the homepage answers HEAD successfully. Such a request cannot inspect reviewed paragraphs, agreement between structured data and visible titles, or a download replaced by an error page.

Suppose a deployment monitor probes a report URL. A successful status and plausible content type suggest the resource exists, but the reader’s GET may involve additional routing, caching, or generation. This hypothetical example does not allege that the methods must be implemented differently. Even a correct HEAD implementation does not inspect the report’s business content.

Match the reader’s access conditions

Check public resources as a public reader would. For protected resources, use the appropriate permission scope: administrator access alone cannot prove ordinary-user access. Parameters, language routes, and negotiation can select different representations. One default URL is not evidence for every translation. These are proposed validation arrangements, not tests performed on the hypothetical service.

Choose body checks appropriate to the resource. Articles need visible-title and complete-text checks; interfaces need structural and state checks; downloads need content identity and media-type checks. HEAD can precede a large download check, but verifying a range is not verifying the entire file. State that limitation.

Name what the check establishes

HEAD remains useful for lightweight metadata queries. Separate resource probing, body consistency, authorization, and business outcomes so a successful indicator has a clear scope.

When HEAD and GET disagree, record the method, status, headers, identity, and time before investigating generation or cache paths. A successful probe alone does not establish recovery, and every difference is not necessarily a cache defect. See cache validation and freshness for the effect of conditional requests.
