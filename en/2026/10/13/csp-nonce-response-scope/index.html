Scope a CSP nonce to the HTTP response that carries it

Generate response-scoped CSP nonces and keep policy, approved tags and caches consistent.

M Midnight Engineering notes on development, quality, security, delivery, and operations. View Author Profile
Updated 2026-10-13 · Estimated read 5 min
Translations
zh-CN en

Article

A CSP nonce belongs to the HTTP response that generates it, rather than a build-time constant reused indefinitely. The policy header and authorized script or style tags must agree on that response’s value. A predictable fixed string is not a general safety marker.

Match within a response, refresh between responses

The MDN CSP guide requires an unpredictable nonce that differs for every HTTP response, with matching values in the policy and approved tags. It also warns against indiscriminately adding nonces to every script tag, which could authorize injected scripts. This article explains the documented mechanism, not a website security assessment.

Imagine a template generating a nonce at build time and returning it to every visitor. The page may work, but its lifetime does not meet the documented requirement. Conversely, an edge layer might refresh the header while returning cached markup with an old value. These are hypothetical design risks, not reproduced cache failures or attacks.

Multiple approved tags in one response can share its nonce. The distinction is consistency within that response and unpredictable fresh values between responses. Identify generation, injection, and caching points instead of treating a nonce attribute in source as proof of correctness.

Include caching in the policy design

Across caches and proxies, verify that the header and body form a consistent response. A working script in one browser does not cover every cache path. Broadly permitting inline execution to restore functionality would also change the original permission boundary.

For suitable static content, the guide describes hash-based authorization. The hash must match the actual content, and content changes require policy maintenance. Hashes and nonces are not unconditional interchangeable switches: deployment and content behavior determine their maintenance requirements.

Explain what is authorized

Add nonces only to deliberately approved content, not blindly throughout unknown HTML. Permission to execute does not establish correct business logic or replace output handling, authorization, and dependency review.

A proposed review checks fresh response values, policy-tag agreement, cache hits, and blocked unauthorized content. No execution results are supplied here. Reporting can support rollout investigation, but reporting and enforcement have different consequences; see CSP reporting and enforcement.
