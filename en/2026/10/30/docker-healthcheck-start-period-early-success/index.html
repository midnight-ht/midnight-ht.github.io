Why can Docker become unhealthy before start-period ends?

Follow failure, early success and subsequent failures while separating probe scheduling, Engine support, checked readiness and recovery actions.

M Midnight Engineering notes on development, quality, security, delivery, and operations. View Author Profile
Updated 2026-10-30 · Estimated read 11 min

Article

Key takeaways

  • Early startup success makes subsequent consecutive failures count.
  • Frequency, timeout and retry count control different conditions.
  • A checked condition is separate from business readiness and recovery actions.

Docker start-period is not an unconditional countdown during which a container cannot become unhealthy. A successful check during that period changes subsequent accounting: the container is considered started, and later consecutive failures count toward retries. Inspect the sequence before merely extending the window.

A failure grace is not delayed checking

The Dockerfile HEALTHCHECK reference defines a health status separate from normal container state. It begins as starting, becomes healthy after a successful check, and becomes unhealthy after the required consecutive failures. Failures during initialization grace do not count, but early success makes subsequent failures count. The online original was checked on October 30, 2026; no container or timing experiment was run.

Start-period therefore does not mean sleeping until the deadline before checking, or immunity from unhealthy for the entire duration. Checks run and success is adopted. An endpoint briefly returning success before essential data finishes loading can end the expected grace too early.

TCP connectivity, a rendered page and required data readiness can be different conditions. Explain which success justifies normal failure accounting. A cheap check is not automatically sufficient; conversely it should not perform expensive writes or repeatedly initialize the application.

Follow one brief success through the sequence

Assume an image supplies a read-only /usr/local/bin/check-ready script. This unexecuted fragment requires separately prepared application and image contents; the declaration does not create the script.

HEALTHCHECK --interval=10s --timeout=2s \
  --start-period=60s --start-interval=5s --retries=3 \
  CMD ["/usr/local/bin/check-ready"]

Suppose two checks fail during startup, one succeeds, then three consecutive checks fail. The earlier failures are covered by grace. After success, subsequent failures count normally. Unhealthy can therefore be possible before sixty seconds have elapsed. This describes an outcome sequence, not exact wall-clock timestamps.

Scheduling also includes check duration and timeout behavior. The reference relates intervals to completion of the previous check. Multiplying retries by an interval does not establish a precise recovery deadline. Record starts, completions, exits and transitions; container age alone omits the decisive early success.

A useful proposed control holds all options fixed while changing whether startup ever succeeds. A never-successful initialization and success-followed-by-failure sequence should differ. Changing the image, script and timing simultaneously would obscure the cause. These controls were not run.

Extend the period or fix the readiness condition?

A genuinely slower initialization that has not passed the necessary check may justify a different grace duration, based on observed startup behavior. If the probe declares success prematurely, a longer period may not help: the early-success condition still ends failure immunity. Inspect what success actually means first.

Start-interval controls startup-check frequency and requires Engine 25.0 or later according to the reference. Do not hand that option to older versions without checking support. Interval, timeout and retries control different variables. More frequent checking is not inherently more reliable, and longer timeouts do not establish business recovery.

A health transition also does not promise automatic restart in standalone Docker. Health failure versus restart conditions addresses that separate contract. An external controller needs its own checks for state consumption, recovery actions and interrupted work.

Does a fixed sleep prove initialization is complete?

No. Time passing does not establish data, permissions or dependency readiness. A check that merely sleeps and succeeds can create an unsupported green status; sleeping beyond timeout can instead fail the check. Choose an observable, bounded condition related to local serving capability.

Some dependency failures are recoverable by the application and should not automatically remove every replica. Coordinate probing and recovery without pretending one endpoint or threshold fits all services. Identify indispensable initialization conditions before copying a sixty-second setting.

Proposed validation records image identity, Engine version, resolved health configuration and read-only results. Cover never successful, early success followed by consecutive failures, an isolated later failure, and timeout. Observe how success interrupts the failure sequence and what any recovery controller actually does. Without that evidence, the configuration expresses intent rather than proven protection or recovery.

A healthy local read-only check can coexist with a business operation failing on external permissions. Healthy establishes the checked condition, not every user path. Expanding a periodic check to every dependency can itself add cost and coupling; keep its purpose explicit and validate other paths separately.

Probe diagnostics are bounded and should remain short and nonsensitive. They are not a complete incident archive. Correlate controlled application logs with container identity and time instead of interpreting absent output as proof that no check ran.
