Why do prices and actions disappear after text-spacing changes?

Apply all four spacing overrides and inspect clipping, overlap, task completion, and writing-system exceptions.

M Midnight Engineering notes on development, quality, security, delivery, and operations. View Author Profile
Updated 2026-10-25 · Estimated read 13 min
Translations
zh-CN en

Article

Key takeaways

  • Apply all applicable spacing overrides without losing content or function.
  • Check neighboring overlap and the task after fixing fixed heights.
  • Keep writing-system exceptions, text resizing, and reflow distinct.

A registration card may fit its price by default yet clip the amount when a user increases line height and tracking. WCAG 2.2 asks whether content and functionality survive a defined spacing override. Apply the four settings together and complete registration; changing the default line height alone does not perform that check.

What do the four overrides change?

W3C's explanation of SC 1.4.12 specifies line height of at least 1.5 times the font size, spacing after paragraphs of at least twice the font size, letter spacing of at least 0.12 times the font size, and word spacing of at least 0.16 times the font size. In markup content supporting these properties, applying all four without changing other style properties must not lose content or functionality. These are override conditions, not mandatory default typography or a requirement to provide a settings button.

The properties consume space in different directions. Wider tracking can wrap a label; larger line height increases the height of multiline text; paragraph spacing pushes the following block down. A fixed-height card with clipped overflow may then hide a price, condition, or action. A text node remaining in the DOM, where a checker can still find it, does not establish that a user can read or operate it.

Start repairs at the container imposing the wrong constraint. Letting content increase the container's height usually respects the user's spacing preference better than shrinking the font. If the initial presentation must be limited, a clear, operable expansion path should reveal the complete content. Do not assume that an unspecified page elsewhere compensates for a missing price, consequence, or error required for the current decision.

How would you check a complete registration card?

Consider an event card containing a title, two paragraphs of conditions, a price, and a confirmation button. This is a hypothetical acceptance exercise, not an executed page test. Establish a baseline with ordinary content, then substitute a long title, a location description containing English, and a validation-error state. Record whether the conditions, amount, and action are available, rather than merely capturing the card's border.

At the same viewport, apply all four overrides while leaving the font size and other properties unchanged. If two lines become four and push the price into a clipped region, the expected result is that the user can still read the conditions, identify the amount, and register. It is not that the card retains its original height. Let the card and list row grow, revise neighboring layout, and prevent the action from covering the last line. Recheck the error state, whose additional message must fit too.

Read from the heading through the conditions and amount, activate the action, and inspect the review page. It should identify the same event and amount. Returning to the list should not reintroduce clipping through a changed state. This joins typography to the task: a working button does not compensate for an invisible amount needed to make the decision.

Simply exposing overflow is a counterexample. Text might become visible while overlapping another card's price or button. The user may no longer know which amount belongs to which event, or may be unable to use the next action. Shrinking the font to keep the original height is another poor substitute: it changes other styling and may defeat the user's reason for increasing spacing. Preserve the override conditions while repairing layout, then inspect the relationships between adjacent content.

Can a dialog and toolbar share one screenshot check?

They need different task checks. Dialogs often have their own scrolling area, while toolbars arrange controls horizontally. After dialog text grows, check whether the final paragraph, close action, and confirmation control remain reachable. If only the background document scrolls while the dialog's bottom stays outside the viewport, adding a scrollbar somewhere has not fixed the route. A scrolling region is a possible choice, provided users can discover and use it and necessary content is reachable.

When toolbar labels grow, inspect wrapping, overlap, and displaced actions. More space, another arrangement, or a clearly discoverable equivalent entry may be appropriate depending on task frequency and the relationship between actions. Replacing every text button with an unexplained icon merely changes the recognition problem and introduces other naming and comprehension concerns.

Save evidence by component state: before-and-after presentation, effective text styles, viewport, long-content samples, keyboard reachability, and pointer activation. Larger visible gaps do not establish that all four settings reached every text element. Inheritance, inline rules, or component isolation can leave some text unchanged. Inspect rendered styles before evaluating adaptation, so an ineffective override cannot create an empty pass.

Does Chinese text need artificial spaces for the test?

No. The criterion allows properties unused by a particular language and writing system to be handled according to the properties that do apply. Do not insert spaces into Chinese sentences solely to manufacture a word-spacing exercise. A Chinese page may still contain English passages or other spaced text; inspect the actual content instead of exempting the entire site. The exception concerns writing systems, not a preference for a compact layout.

This check also does not replace text resizing or reflow checks. Spacing overrides hold other styling constant; zoom and narrow viewports have their own conditions. They may expose the same fixed-height defect, but passing one does not establish the others. The site's target-size article examines activation areas and their neighbors; a sufficiently large button does not establish that its label survives changed spacing.

Bind the conclusion to the page version, states, and effective override settings examined. Start with a card containing the longest necessary information, complete its task under the four overrides, and then extend the exercise to dialogs and toolbars. If content is still missing, identify the exact text and clipping container. “The registration amount falls into the clipped region after the override” is an inspectable defect; “the page feels crowded” is not a repair specification.
