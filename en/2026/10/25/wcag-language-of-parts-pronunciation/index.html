Why does a screen reader keep the wrong language for a quoted sentence?

Inspect passage boundaries, language inheritance, editing, and accessible names without promising voice-switching support.

M Midnight Engineering notes on development, quality, security, delivery, and operations. View Author Profile
Updated 2026-10-25 · Estimated read 14 min
Translations
zh-CN en

Article

Key takeaways

  • Mark meaningful language boundaries rather than classifying every letter.
  • Recheck inherited language after editing, copying, and translation.
  • Verify mixed-language controls with the target assistive-technology combination.

When a Chinese notice quotes “Please bring your registration confirmation.”, a screen reader may continue using Chinese pronunciation rules. Check whether the quotation’s local language can be determined programmatically, rather than only setting the page language. Markup supplies presentation and pronunciation information; it does not install a missing voice or translate incorrect text.

What do page language and local language each describe?

W3C's explanation of SC 3.1.2 requires the human language of passages and phrases to be programmatically determinable, with exceptions for proper names, technical terms, indeterminate words, and words or phrases adopted into the surrounding language. Even one word can fall within scope unless an exception applies. A language-bearing passage is the relevant unit, not the presence of Latin letters alone.

The main page language supplies the default. Local markup identifies where that default changes. A Chinese service page quoting an English instruction should give the quotation appropriate language information. Marking a container that includes both the Chinese introduction and English quotation as English creates an incorrect inherited language for the introduction. Boundaries matter more than the number of attributes in the source.

Implementation has limits. W3C’s note concerns situations or operating modes that announce an element through its computed accessible name, such as an isolated links list in screen readers. The name is a flat string, so language changes within its parts may not reach assistive technology even when the underlying markup is correct. The relevant boundary is how a name is computed and announced, rather than a general rule for a particular control category. Moving the language attribute to the element itself may help simple cases without working across every user-agent and assistive-technology combination. Evaluation of this criterion must inspect the underlying language markup rather than judging solely by spoken switching; usability on the target device still needs separate verification.

Where should a bilingual notice switch language?

Consider a hypothetical notice: a Chinese paragraph gives the meeting time, an English quotation says “Please bring your registration confirmation.”, and another Chinese paragraph provides a contact route. This is not an executed screen-reader test. The page has Chinese as its main language, the complete quotation has English language markup, and the final paragraph continues to inherit Chinese. There is no need to split every word around punctuation into a separate element.

The editor first establishes the quotation's exact boundaries. The template preserves the corresponding attribute. Inspect the rendered DOM and inherited language, then read through the time, quotation, and contact information in order. The expected structural result is correct language information. Pronunciation switching depends on support in the selected combination; missing voices or unsupported switching should be recorded specifically. Hearing a different voice is not the only evidence for programmatic language.

Extend the quotation to two English sentences and check that markup covers both. An editor may split one quotation into several nodes while preserving the language only on the first. It still looks continuous, but the second part inherits Chinese. Copying may preserve appearance while discarding attributes. Test creation, subsequent editing, and the published body, rather than relying on one editor preview.

If the quotation is replaced by a Chinese translation, remove or update the obsolete English markup. The attribute belongs to the current text, not permanently to that location in the template. A multilingual editorial system can preserve fragment language explicitly, but the article's overall language should not be assigned blindly to every embedded passage. A Chinese article can quote another language.

Does every brand name or acronym need its own lang attribute?

Do not classify text letter by letter. Proper names and technical terms are listed exceptions, but the reason still matters. A product name in Chinese technical prose differs from an entire English instruction. Mentioning a brand does not make the whole instruction a proper name. Where a word's language is indeterminate, avoid assigning one through guesswork.

First identify a continuous passage in an actual language, then consider applicable exceptions. Additional markup for a proper name may help pronunciation in a tested environment, but it should not be described as a universal obligation to switch every such word. Fragmenting text excessively also creates maintenance risk when an editor rearranges pieces with conflicting inheritance.

The site's label-in-name article asks what text enters a control's accessible name. This article asks how the text's language is presented. A correct name does not establish correct language, or the reverse. A button pronounced correctly as “Save” can still have a mismatched hidden name meaning “Send”.

Can a child element fix foreign-language text in a hidden name?

Do not assume so. Visible content, the computed accessible name, and the description can come from different sources. Inspecting one English child node may miss the name spoken in the current assistive-technology mode. Flat-name limitations concern more than attribute strings: they can also arise after a name is computed from marked-up content. A child’s language annotation therefore does not establish that segmented language survives name announcement. Record the name’s source, final value, and operating mode, inspect the underlying language markup, and then check output in the target combination.

For an essential action with recurring mixed-language compatibility problems, consider wording that is clear to the primary audience, or provide full bilingual explanation in adjacent readable text. Do not make an action's name completely different from its visible wording merely to produce a voice change in one test. A pronunciation repair must still preserve the action's object and visible promise.

A proposed verification set can begin with a complete foreign-language quotation, a proper name, and a mixed-language control. Record the main language, local boundaries, inherited values, name sources, browser and assistive-technology versions, and installed voices. Check attribute preservation after editing too. Automated inspection can identify omissions, but cannot classify every word's language or certify pronunciation. Establish reliable boundaries first, then report usability within the environment actually examined.
