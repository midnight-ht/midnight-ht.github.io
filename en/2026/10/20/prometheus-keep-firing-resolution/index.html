Why a recovered metric can leave an alert firing

The alert may remain firing after its condition stops matching. Read the retention window and scrape health separately.

M Midnight Engineering notes on development, quality, security, delivery, and operations. View Author Profile
Updated 2026-10-20 · Estimated read 13 min
Translations
zh-CN en

Article

Key takeaways

  • for delays firing; keep_firing_for delays resolution after the last match.
  • Missing data can stop an expression from matching without proving health.
  • Record the rule version, labels, last match, and actual resolution time.

An alert can remain firing after a Prometheus expression stops matching on the current evaluation. With keep_firing_for, Prometheus keeps that alert instance firing for a specified period after the condition was last met. The retained state is a configured window, not a fresh measurement that the fault persists. When a dashboard looks healthy but an alert still says firing, distinguish the expression result from the alert state. The Prometheus alerting rules documentation describes this behavior directly.

Two timers on opposite sides of firing

The official example uses for: 10m and keep_firing_for: 5m. for requires an expression output element to remain active for the configured duration before it becomes firing; beforehand it is pending. keep_firing_for holds a firing alert for five minutes after its condition last matched. Without for, an alert can become active on the first matching evaluation. Without keep_firing_for, it deactivates on the first evaluation where the condition does not match, assuming any prior for requirement has been satisfied. One timer delays entry into firing; the other delays exit. They do not substitute for each other.

Consider an illustrative, unexecuted timeline with one-minute evaluations. A label group exceeds the threshold from 10:00 through 10:10 and eventually passes a ten-minute for requirement. Suppose 10:12 is the last matching evaluation, and the element disappears from the expression result at 10:13. A five-minute keep_firing_for allows the alert to remain firing until the retention window ends. The exact transition depends on evaluation scheduling, boundary time, and the identity of the series; this example does not promise a production alert will resolve at precisely 10:17. An incident record should distinguish the last threshold breach, first nonmatching evaluation, and actual alert resolution.

Why add the window? A series may hover around a threshold or disappear briefly during a scrape failure. Without retention, the alert can resolve and fire again in quick succession. Holding the state can reduce that churn and prevent downstream systems from treating a one-evaluation gap as a completed recovery. The cost is real: a genuine recovery is reported later. If resolution drives an automation, a ticket closure, or a customer status page, the delay belongs in the design and acceptance criteria rather than hidden in a rule file.

Missing data is not evidence of recovery

Imagine the expression depends on an instance metric and a failed scrape makes the series temporarily absent. If the expression no longer emits that label group, the rule sees a nonmatching result. That alone does not establish that the service is healthy. keep_firing_for can bridge a short gap, but it is no replacement for monitoring scrape health and absent data. Compare the raw metric, target scrape status, and complete alert labels during an incident. Otherwise, the operator can misread a retained firing state as continuing failure or a later resolved state as proved recovery; neither inference is justified by the state transition alone.

Labels matter because Prometheus tracks alert instances for expression output elements. A service alert may include instance or region labels; the last match for one element does not summarize every region. Changes to the expression or labels can also alter the identity of an instance. Review the full label set, rule group, expression, evaluation interval, and configured retention duration. Keep this separate from Alertmanager grouping, inhibition, silences, and notification repeat intervals. Those mechanisms affect what people receive, while the rule's keep_firing_for controls the Prometheus alert state.

Choose a window for the actual duty cycle

A short window may help when one missing scrape or a brief oscillation would otherwise produce repeated resolve-and-fire events. It can be harmful when an alert is used to trigger automated rollback of a mitigation or close an incident. Pick a duration based on evaluation frequency, observed noise, and required recovery reporting. Copying five minutes from an example is not an operational decision. Too long delays a truthful resolution; too short leaves multi-evaluation flapping intact. Different alerts in the same service need not share the same value.

A useful test plan supplies controlled input sequences: a sustained breach long enough to pass for, a one-evaluation recovery followed by another breach, a genuine recovery longer than the retention window, and a gap of missing samples. Inspect pending, firing, and resolved state for each complete label group. Then check whether downstream notification settings add another delay. If automation reacts to a resolved event, verify which component emitted it and what evidence of health that event represents. This article did not run Prometheus or a live rule test; the timeline explains the documented mechanism.

Keep the rule version with an incident record. If someone changed keep_firing_for during the event, today's rule file cannot explain yesterday's resolution time. Save the loaded rule, its modification time, and the relevant labels. A notification interface may collapse several instances into one message; expand them before deciding whether one instance is in its retention window while another still breaches the condition.

Automation should not infer “continue scaling” solely from firing, or “recovery work complete” solely from resolved. A scaler needs its own target measurements and guardrails. If it consumes alert events, its design must account for this hysteresis and for repeated delivery. Then the retention window remains an explicit noise-control choice rather than an unexplained delay propagated to every dependent system.

Put current value, expression output, alert state, last matching time, and notification state side by side in an incident record. Operators can then avoid two premature claims: that a firing label proves the underlying fault continues, or that a curve below threshold proves the incident is fully closed. The site's release acceptance review similarly separates a process signal from actual behavior. Here the practical gain is narrower: give each alert state its exact rule meaning.
