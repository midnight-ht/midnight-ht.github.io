Why does exceeding a Prometheus rule limit discard every result?

Understand whole-rule output loss, cleared alerts and evaluation errors, including why old queryable samples do not establish fresh success.

M Midnight Engineering notes on development, quality, security, delivery, and operations. View Author Profile
Updated 2026-10-26 · Estimated read 11 min
Translations
zh-CN en

Article

Key takeaways

  • A group-configured limit discards an over-limit rule's entire output rather than truncating it.
  • Cleared alert states do not prove business recovery; check evaluation health independently.
  • No stale markers are written for this error, so visible old samples do not prove recent success.

A recording rule grows from two services to three while its group limit remains two. Instead of losing one service, the rule produces no new output. Prometheus discards all series from an over-limit rule, and an alerting rule also clears its own alert states. Inspect evaluation errors before interpreting missing business signals; the limit is not a first-few-results truncation policy.

Configured per group, applied to the offending rule

The recording rule documentation defines a group-level limit on alerts produced by alerting rules and series produced by recording rules. Zero means unlimited. Exceeding it discards the offending rule's whole output. Per-group configuration should not be read as a shared pool that selects a subset from the combined output of every rule, or as a statement that every other rule is necessarily discarded.

This protection does not rewrite an expression into meaningful business sampling. If the requirement is the services with the highest error rates, explicitly define ranking and dimensions in the expression and review monitoring for excluded objects. Failure due to a limit is not that selection policy.

Three results with a limit of two

Consider an unexecuted recording rule that returns one error-rate series for each service. At a particular evaluation it produces A, B and C, while the group limit is two. A truncation interpretation would expect two services to remain, perhaps selected by ordering. The documented result is different: all three outputs from this rule are discarded and evaluation records an error. It is incorrect to promise that A and B remain while C disappears.

C could be a newly launched service, or a label change could have split an existing identity. The first calls for revisiting expected scale and budget. The second calls for tracing labels, particularly unbounded request identities or temporary instances. Inspect the expression's result at the relevant timestamp before increasing the limit.

Preserve three evidence layers: input series, the expression result at the evaluation time, and the rule's error or status. Missing recorded output does not establish that scraping failed. If another rule consumes this metric, determine whether it sees absence, an older sample or its own evaluation problem. Explain each layer against its actual query time.

Cleared alerts do not establish recovery

The documentation says an over-limit alerting rule clears active, pending and inactive alerts for that rule. A pending alert cannot be assumed to retain its earlier waiting progress across the failure. The business incident may remain while the rule cannot produce valid alerts within its constraint. Monitoring evaluation health and monitoring the business condition are separate responsibilities.

A weak operational response is announcing recovery because a business alert disappeared without checking evaluation success. Review the error and raw business signal first. Notification behavior also depends on the actual downstream system; this article does not promise which message Alertmanager or another receiver will send.

Moving from service-level to tenant-level alerts needs an advance budget. Separate tenant states can hit the limit; aggregating them away may remove identities that responders need. Decide using the scope on which someone can act. A global healthy result cannot establish that every tenant is healthy.

Does exceeding the limit write stale markers?

The documentation explicitly says it does not: the event is an evaluation error, so no stale markers are written. Missing new output therefore does not give consumers an immediate explicit series-ending marker. Whether an older sample remains queryable depends on query time, lookback and actual data state. A query returning a value is not proof of a newly successful evaluation.

This can make charts look calmer than evaluation health. Show the latest successful evaluation time and current error state, or provide an independent freshness signal. Verify the actual behavior with the deployed Prometheus version and query. No rule server was run or stale-marker behavior observed for this article.

Choose the objective before changing the limit

Expected business growth can justify a larger limit after reviewing storage, response workload and downstream query costs. Unexpected dimension growth calls for expression or label repair. Selecting a few important objects calls for an explicit selection policy and coverage for excluded ones. These are different decisions.

Proposed validation covers fewer than, exactly, and more than the configured number of outputs for both recording and alerting rules. Include pre-existing alert states, inspect errors and subsequent recovery, and observe unaffected rules instead of generalizing one rule's outcome to its entire group. The example numbers and procedures remain unexecuted.

The recording-rule label contract covers output identity. Establish what each series represents, set an interpretable scale guard, and monitor the guard itself. Otherwise protection against excessive output can remove the failure signal on which operators rely.
