Requirements 2026-10-18

Use collapsed details for help, keep action conditions visible

Separate supplementary help from information users need before deciding, and make disclosure controls descriptive.

M Midnight Engineering notes on development, quality, security, delivery, and operations. View Author Profile
Updated 2026-10-18 · Estimated read 8 min
Translations
zh-CN en

Article

A registration page can look cleaner after moving its cancellation deadline into “More information”. Yet that deadline may be needed before anyone decides to register. Collapsed details suit supplementary help. Information most users need before acting should be visible without requiring them to discover and open another section.

Present somewhere does not mean seen

The GOV.UK details guidance recommends the component for information only some users need, and advises against hiding material needed by the majority. This is guidance for choosing a component. It does not establish that placing information in a disclosure means users have been adequately informed.

Imagine an event where self-service cancellation closes the day before it starts. This is a hypothetical scenario, with no registration data behind it. The deadline could change someone’s decision, so it belongs near the registration action. Help finding a booking reference or completing an unusual identity-document field may suit a disclosure. The distinction is the decision the text supports, rather than its length.

An overcrowded page still needs work. Remove repetition, shorten explanations, or reconsider the steps. Hiding a necessary condition only changes its visibility. A complex rule may need a short, accurate conclusion in the main content and a longer explanation beneath it. That conclusion must retain the conditions that could change a choice; “see rules below” does not do that work.

Separate conditions from explanations

During review, temporarily cover the collapsed section. Can users still judge whether the service applies to them, what it costs them, and what their next action means? If omitting a passage could reverse the decision, consider moving it into the main content. This is an editorial method inferred from the guidance, not a measured usability threshold.

On a hypothetical upload page, everyone needs the accepted file types and any size limit. The reason a format is unsupported may be supplementary. If some users can only supply alternative evidence, give them a recognizable route. A vague “Other information” label should not require those users to search every help section. Where their situation is already known, a relevant direct prompt may be more appropriate.

The majority rule is not the whole judgment. A condition affecting a minority may still deserve prominent treatment when its consequence is difficult to undo. That is this article’s product recommendation, beyond the component document’s short rule. It calls for relevant people to see relevant warnings, rather than putting every exception on the first screen.

Make the disclosure understandable

GOV.UK recommends short, descriptive disclosure text. “Help me find my document number” explains the contents more clearly than “More”. The control should not resemble the next step, or be required just to understand the current question. Several peer sections also warrant another look at the content structure; details, accordions, and tabs should not be treated as interchangeable skins.

Possible validation tasks include making a decision with details initially closed, checking which restrictions the participant understood, and observing whether people needing help find it. Target-device checks should also cover keyboard use, assistive technology, and reading order after expansion. None of these tests was performed here, so no reduction in mistakes is claimed.

The site’s form question protocol asks why an answer is collected. Disclosure design determines when its explanation appears. Keep choice-changing conditions near the action, then give supporting material a clear entrance. A shorter page still needs to explain what the user is agreeing to.
