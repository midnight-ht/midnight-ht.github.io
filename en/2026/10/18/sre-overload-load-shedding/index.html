Reject overload without turning recovery into the next traffic surge

Connect bounded admission, deadlines, truthful degradation and retry budgets, then readmit backlog progressively with explicit side-effect and fairness checks.

M Midnight Engineering notes on development, quality, security, delivery, and operations. View Author Profile
Updated 2026-10-29 · Estimated read 12 min

zh-CN en

Article

Key takeaways

  • Accepted requests and useful completions are different objectives.
  • Rejection cost, degraded outcomes and chain-wide retries need one policy.
  • Progressive readmission needs backlog-age evidence and duplicate-operation reconciliation.

Overload protection should preserve useful work that can still finish within its deadline, rather than accept every request. Bounded queues, cheap rejection, explicit degradation and client retry behavior belong in one design. Otherwise accumulated work and retries can create another surge as soon as protection is lifted. Verify business outcomes and feedback convergence together.

Is a result still useful after its caller gives up?

The Google SRE chapter on cascading failures discusses queueing latency, memory use, work that outlives caller deadlines and early, inexpensive failure. These are published historical engineering methods. Sources were rechecked for this October 29, 2026 rewrite; no load test was run and no capacity threshold is established for this site or another service.

Assume an interactive caller waits three seconds while the server queue takes ten seconds before execution starts. A correct eventual result may arrive after the caller has abandoned it. Continued admission occupies queues, connections, memory and dependencies while making other viable requests late. Accepted requests and useful completions are different objectives.

Deadlines must cross the call chain. A downstream service using a fresh full timeout may continue expensive work after upstream cancellation. Cancellation propagation, interruptibility and existing side effects require implementation evidence. An HTTP timeout does not prove a database operation or external job stopped.

Follow an admission decision through its outcomes

Consider an unexecuted design where interactive reads and background exports share workers. Product policy allows exports to wait while interactive reads need a bounded, explicit outcome. Ingress identifies work class and remaining deadline, admits viable work into a finite queue and rejects requests outside capacity or time budgets. Execution checks the deadline again because queued work can expire while waiting.

Priorities come from explicit business agreements, not interface names guessed during an incident. A delayed export needs persisted state, a visible reason and cancellation or status lookup. It is neither silently discarded nor declared complete. A rejected read did not obtain its expected result. A cache-based degraded response must communicate its age and scope rather than impersonating complete current data.

If an export already wrote part of a file, it is no longer “not started.” Query its stable task identity before deciding to resume, rebuild or clean up. For payments or stock updates, distinguish pre-execution rejection from a response lost after a side effect. Recovery must not manufacture duplicate business operations.

Rejection also consumes capacity

The Google SRE overload chapter notes that rejection has a cost, potentially close to normal processing for simple services, and discusses client-side throttling. Adding a rejection branch does not establish protection. Rejecting only after expensive authentication, parsing and database work may be too late.

A high-level gate can act early but may not know each operation's cost. A gate near the constrained resource knows more but has already incurred upstream work. Coordinate layers so independently retrying and rejecting components do not multiply attempts without a chain-wide owner.

Request count and queue length alone can hide major cost differences between small reads and heavy exports. Dependency slowdown and workload changes can invalidate an old threshold. Retain trigger evidence, ownership and exit criteria instead of turning an emergency number into permanent policy.

Can retries multiply the rejected load?

Yes. Immediate retries at several call layers can turn one operation into many competing attempts. Define eligible failures, bounded attempts, an overall deadline, backoff and jitter, and who owns the operation's total budget. Jitter disperses timing; it does not bound volume. An exhausted deadline should stop attempts even when the nominal retry count remains unused.

See 429 responses and retry budgets for caller waiting and stopping rules. Here they must be tested with admission control. Successfully sending a rejection does not establish that actual clients reduced their attempts. Uncontrolled third-party clients also require independent server protection.

Readmit accumulated work without another surge

Recovery is not necessarily an immediate switch from rejection to unrestricted admission. Retry timers, schedulers and backlog replay can all resume together. One candidate policy readmits in batches, prioritizes work that remains valuable, preserves retry budgets and increases admission according to useful completion and dependency pressure. Define a reversible condition that stops expansion when pressure returns.

Re-evaluate old work. Expired reads may be useless to replay, while delayed exports can remain necessary. Product rules decide cancellation, coalescing and delay. Progressive recovery also needs fairness and maximum-wait boundaries so emergency priority does not become permanent starvation.

Proposed validation covers ordinary demand, bursts, sustained overload and recovery. Observe useful completion, explicit rejection, timeouts, queue delay, dependency pressure and client attempts. Add lost feedback, partial side effects and backlog readmission. No throughput or latency results are reported. A short queue can mean widespread failure, and lower CPU can mean an unusable service. Correct outcomes, stable resources and converging feedback are needed to support recovery.

Separate fresh demand, client retries and backlog replay in recovery evidence, and track the age of the oldest waiting task. Normal total completion can coexist with starvation of old work. Before manually releasing a batch, reconcile its identity and completed operations to avoid admitting it twice. Decide on the next batch after observing the current one rather than clearing every scheduler restriction at once.
