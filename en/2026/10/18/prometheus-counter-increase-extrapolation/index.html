Why Prometheus increase can be fractional for integer counters

Audit a paper calculation of 6.25 against a fixed implementation, then distinguish extrapolation, zero constraints, resets, aggregation, and reconciliation.

Updated 2026-10-28 · Estimated read 13 min
Translations

Article

Key takeaways

  • Integer increments can yield fractional window estimates without proving duplicate counting.
  • Exact factors depend on samples, boundary gaps, zero constraints, and implementation conditions.
  • Neither display rounding nor longer windows turn estimates into individual event records.

A counter that increments once per request can produce a noninteger increase over the last five minutes. Prometheus estimates a window increase from scraped snapshots and extrapolation; a fraction alone is not evidence of duplicate counting. First list window boundaries and sample times, then inspect resets, series identity, and presentation. Changing display precision does not change the measurement.

A window estimate is not a list of individual events

The Prometheus increase documentation describes automatic adjustment for counter resets and extrapolation across the selected range. Integer increments can therefore yield noninteger results. This article concerns ordinary range selectors and float counters, excluding native histograms and additional range modifiers.

Scrapes store cumulative snapshots at particular times, not every business event. A five-minute query need not have samples exactly at its endpoints. Counting individual ledger events and estimating an increase from snapshots have different inputs. Compare scope, time boundaries, and missing-data conditions before diagnosing duplicate instrumentation from disagreement.

A paper example with checkable conditions

Let evaluation occur at second 300 with range (0,300]. The current querying basics reference defines ordinary ranges as left-open and right-closed: a sample exactly on the left boundary is excluded and one on the right is included. Use five hypothetical samples: (30,100), (90,101), (150,102), (210,103), and (270,105).

No monitoring instance ingested these inputs and no query was run. The exact arithmetic below follows the official Prometheus v3.5.0 extrapolatedRate implementation, without calling that version current. These are float-counter samples with no decrease and an initial value well away from zero.

The sampled interval is 240 seconds, the observed increase is 5, and four intervals average 60 seconds each. That implementation's boundary threshold is 1.1 times the average, or 66 seconds. Both endpoint gaps are 30 seconds, below the threshold, so this example extends to both window boundaries. The zero-point duration is 240 multiplied by 100/5, or 4800 seconds, exceeding the starting gap; it does not constrain this extension.

The factor is consequently 300/240, and the estimated increase is 5 multiplied by 1.25, or 6.25. That does not mean observing six and a quarter individual requests. It is the estimate for this window and these snapshots. Do not apply the same factor universally: sparse data, distant boundaries, or an active zero-point constraint require the actual implementation rules rather than automatic division of window duration by sampled duration.

What endpoint subtraction loses after a reset

Consider another counter that rises from 100 to 103, resets to zero, then rises to 5. Subtracting initial 100 from final 5 produces a negative value that does not describe accumulated requests. Increase accounts for observed decreases as resets. A reset and subsequent growth entirely hidden between scrapes cannot be reconstructed as a complete event history, however.

This counterexample predicts no exact query result because full timestamps and samples have not been specified. It explains why two endpoints are insufficient and why permitted fractions do not certify complete collection. Scrape failures, changed labels, and inappropriate metric types can still undermine the business interpretation even when the function follows its definition.

Preserve reset detection before combining instances. The rate documentation requires rate before aggregation so individual target resets remain detectable. Corresponding window totals should likewise handle each series before summing, rather than combining raw counters from restarting and still-growing instances first. The site's rate-before-sum discussion covers that order. The additional focus here is range extrapolation and an auditable numerical example.

Choose among display precision, recorded rates, and reconciliation

The documentation describes increase as readable shorthand for rate multiplied by window seconds and recommends rate for recording rules, preserving a per-second basis. Displaying an estimated five-minute volume differs from comparing a per-second alert rate. If a recorded rate supports a window-volume display, retain an explicit duration and update labels when that duration changes.

Rounding may improve presentation, but it cannot recover missing events and can hide low-volume variation. Displaying a fractional estimate as zero can suggest no activity. Choose precision for the intended decision and make its estimated nature visible. Exact orders, billing, or task reconciliation require suitable business records; an integer-looking monitoring number does not become a ledger.

Does a longer window guarantee an integer?

No. A longer window changes the range without guaranteeing endpoint-aligned scrapes or removing reset and extrapolation behavior. It may smooth short-lived changes, while a shorter window depends more strongly on available samples. First decide whether the question concerns trends, abrupt changes, or individual events. Do not silently change that question merely to make one fractional result disappear.

Fix evaluation time when comparing dashboards. A panel evaluated at second 300 and another refreshed at 310 both say last five minutes, but their moving ranges can include different samples. Align time and labels before comparing expressions and precision. Their disagreement alone cannot identify an extrapolation defect. Preserve query time rather than only the final displayed number, especially with sparse activity and events near range boundaries.

Proposed verification fixes evaluation time, range, sample type, and labels, retaining complete timestamped snapshots before adding resets and gaps. Compare outputs and annotations. Running this article's inputs on the specified version would produce separate execution evidence; copying the paper value of 6.25 into a test log would not. No monitoring instance was queried here. Explain window, resets, and display precision before deciding whether the chart supports a trend judgment or requires independent business-event evidence.
